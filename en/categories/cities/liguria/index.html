🏖️ Why These Cities Stand Out

  • Genoa blends maritime history with vibrant street life. Its old town is one of the largest medieval centers in Europe.
  • Portofino is the jewel of the Italian Riviera, known for luxury yachts, chic boutiques, and breathtaking coastal walks.
  • Cinque Terre villages are world-famous for their pastel houses perched on cliffs, connected by scenic trails and train routes.
  • Sanremo offers a lively cultural scene, with its annual music festival and elegant Belle Époque architecture.
  • Lerici is quieter but stunning, with sandy beaches and access to the Gulf of Poets.
  • Varazze & Beigua Park combine seaside relaxation with mountain hiking, offering panoramic views over Liguria’s coast.

⚠️ Practical Considerations

  • Crowds: Portofino and Cinque Terre are extremely popular in summer; visiting in spring or autumn is more enjoyable.
  • Costs: Portofino is among the most expensive destinations in Liguria, while Genoa and Lerici are more budget-friendly.
  • Accessibility: Cinque Terre villages are best explored by train or boat, as car access is limited.

✅ Recommendation:

  • For culture and history, start with Genoa.
  • For luxury and romance, choose Portofino.
  • For scenic coastal charm, explore the Cinque Terre villages.
  • For family-friendly seaside relaxation, head to Lerici.
  • For nature and hiking, visit Varazze & Beigua Park.

Gemini

The best cities and towns to visit in Liguria showcase a stunning blend of dramatic cliffs, pastel-hued harbors, and rich maritime history, anchored by the iconic capital of Genoa , the world-famous villages of the Cinque Terre , and the luxurious playground of Portofino . Whether you are seeking upscale coastal glamour, rugged hiking trails, or a deep dive into medieval history, this crescent-shaped Italian region delivers unforgettable experiences along both its eastern and western rivieras.

The Postcard-Perfect Gems of the Eastern Riviera

The Riviera di Levante (Eastern Riviera) is home to Liguria's most dramatic cliffside towns, deep-blue harbors, and legendary coastal walks.

Portofino   This world-famous, crescent-shaped fishing village is the undisputed capital of Italian Riviera glamour. While the waterfront Piazzetta is lined with high-end boutiques and mega-yachts, I highly recommend escaping the crowds by walking up to Castello Brown for a panoramic, 360-degree look at the sparkling gulf.

Camogli   If you want the breathtaking aesthetic of the Cinque Terre with a much more authentic, slow-paced atmosphere, Camogli is my top pick. It features towering, brightly painted sea-front houses and a beautiful pebble beach. Local Insight: This is arguably the best food town on the coast—make sure to grab fresh focaccia col formaggio or dine on local anchovies at a waterfront trattoria.

Vernazza   Widely considered the most striking of the five villages that make up the Cinque Terre National Park, Vernazza features a natural harbor guarded by a medieval watchtower. To see it at its best, arrive early via the scenic Sentiero Azzurro hiking trail from Monterosso al Mare.

Santa Margherita Ligure   This elegant resort town serves as a fantastic, family-friendly alternative to its neighbor, Portofino. It boasts a grand liberty-style palm-fringed promenade, beautiful hotels, and excellent beach clubs. It's a wonderful, lower-stress hub for exploring the Tigullio Gulf.

Sestri Levante   Uniquely built on a narrow peninsula, Sestri Levante is famous for being cradled by two distinct bodies of water: the lively Baia delle Favole (Bay of Fairy Tales) and the romantic, tranquil Baia del Silenzio (Bay of Silence). It remains a favorite retreat for Italian vacationers and offers great snorkeling away from the main international crowds.

The Historic Cultural Hubs & Strategic Bases

If you prefer grand architecture, vibrant city energy, or highly connected train stations to use as a jumping-off point, these major cities are mandatory additions.

Genoa   Liguria's capital city is a historic powerhouse, featuring one of the largest medieval old towns in Europe and the majestic, UNESCO-listed Palazzi dei Rolli. While some areas have a gritty, working port appearance, wandering the narrow caruggi alleys reveals magnificent baroque architecture. Check out the Official Genoa Tourism Site to plan visits to the old harbor and its massive aquarium.

La Spezia   While it lacks the hyper-romantic beach aesthetic of smaller villages, La Spezia is unmatched as a practical and affordable base camp. It features an impressive waterfront palm promenade and an extensive train network that can drop you into the heart of the Cinque Terre in mere minutes.

The Sun-Drenched Riviera di Ponente

The Riviera di Ponente (Western Riviera) extends from Genoa to the French border. It is characterized by wider, sandier beaches, a more relaxed pacing, and deep valleys perfect for ecotourism.

Sanremo   Known as the "City of Flowers," Sanremo balances Belle Époque glamour—complete with an iconic historic casino—with great family utility. Beyond the elegant shopping boulevards, ensure you dive into La Pigna, the city's ancient medieval core made of steep, cascading stone tunnels.

Finale Ligure   This dynamic spot is divided into a lively seaside resort and Finalborgo, a beautifully preserved, walled medieval village located slightly inland. It has evolved into a world-famous capital for outdoor sports, drawing rock climbers and mountain bikers from all over the globe to its limestone hills.

Noli   Once an independent maritime republic, Noli is a hidden medieval gem protected by the impressive Castle of Monte Ursino on the hill above. It boasts a slower, authentic seaside charm that feels completely removed from the hectic summer tourist trail.

Grok · grok-3

Genoa (Genova), the Cinque Terre villages, Portofino, Camogli, Santa Margherita Ligure, Sanremo, Sestri Levante, La Spezia (as a base), Rapallo, and Portovenere consistently rank among the top places to visit in Liguria (the Italian Riviera).

Liguria stretches along the northwestern Italian coast from the French border to Tuscany. It mixes historic port cities, glamorous resorts, colorful cliffside villages, and quieter fishing towns. The region divides into the Riviera di Ponente (west of Genoa, more developed beaches and Belle Époque elegance) and Riviera di Levante (east, more dramatic cliffs and iconic villages).

Top recommendations

Here’s a practical rundown of the standouts:

  • Genoa (Genova) — The regional capital and a former maritime republic. Explore the sprawling medieval caruggi (narrow alleys), UNESCO-listed Palazzi dei Rolli on Via Garibaldi, the historic port, Aquarium, and sample authentic pesto (which originated here). It’s atmospheric, less purely touristy than the coastal villages, and makes a strong cultural base.

  • Cinque Terre (Monterosso al Mare, Vernazza, Corniglia, Manarola, Riomaggiore) — The region’s most famous attraction and a UNESCO World Heritage site. Five colorful villages cling to steep cliffs above the sea, linked by hiking paths (Sentiero Azzurro) and a scenic train. Vernazza and Manarola are often called the most photogenic; Monterosso has the best beach. Expect crowds in peak season—visit early/late or stay overnight for a better experience.

  • Portofino — The glamorous, pastel-hued harbor town synonymous with yachts, celebrities, and high-end boutiques. Small and exclusive; ideal for a half-day or day trip (expensive to stay). Pair it with nearby Santa Margherita Ligure or Camogli.

  • Camogli — A more authentic, less crowded alternative to Portofino. Colorful houses, a working fishing harbor, black-pebble beach, and excellent seafood/focaccia. Great atmosphere, especially in the evening.

  • Santa Margherita Ligure — Elegant and romantic, with seafront villas, Blue Flag beaches, and good connections to Portofino. A comfortable base for the Tigullio Gulf area.

  • Sanremo — Elegant western Riviera resort known as the “City of Flowers.” Palm-lined promenades (Corso Imperatrice), historic casino, Belle Époque architecture, and the medieval La Pigna quarter. Famous for the Sanremo Music Festival.

  • Sestri Levante — Charming town with two beautiful bays (Baia del Silenzio / Bay of Silence and Baia delle Favole). Attractive old town and a good mix of beach and atmosphere.

  • La Spezia — Practical gateway to the Cinque Terre and Gulf of Poets (Portovenere, Lerici). It has its own waterfront promenade, naval museum, and solid food scene; useful as a base rather than a pure sightseeing stop.

  • Other strong contenders:

    • Rapallo — Convenient base with a seafront castle and access to the Portofino area.
    • Portovenere — Dramatic cliffside town at the western tip of the Gulf of Poets, with a striking church and castle; pairs well with the islands of Palmaria, Tino, and Tinetto.
    • Quieter or inland options: Finale Ligure / Finalborgo (medieval walls + beaches), Noli, Chiavari, Albenga, or hill towns like Apricale and Dolceacqua.

Quick tips

  • Best time: Spring (April–June) or early autumn (September–October) for fewer crowds and pleasant weather. Summer is peak season and very busy, especially Cinque Terre and Portofino.
  • Getting around: Trains are excellent along the coast (especially useful for Cinque Terre). Boats connect many villages in good weather. A car helps for the western Riviera and inland villages but parking can be tight in popular spots.
  • Food highlights: Fresh seafood, focaccia, pesto, farinata, and local wines (Vermentino, Sciacchetrà).

Genoa + the eastern Riviera (Portofino/Camogli area + Cinque Terre) covers the classic highlights for most first-time visitors. Add Sanremo or the western coast if you prefer more beach time and fewer crowds. Enjoy the trip!
