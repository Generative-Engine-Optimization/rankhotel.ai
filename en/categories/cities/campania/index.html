Copilot · search

The best cities to visit in Campania are Naples, Amalfi, Pompeii, Caserta, Benevento, and Massa Lubrense (Punta Campanella). Each offers a unique mix of history, culture, and breathtaking landscapes.

🌟 Top Cities & Highlights in Campania

1. Naples

  • Why visit: Vibrant capital of Campania, famous for its historic center (UNESCO site), world-class museums, and authentic pizza.
  • Highlights: Naples National Archaeological Museum, Spaccanapoli, Castel dell’Ovo, and proximity to Mount Vesuvius.
  • Atmosphere: Energetic, chaotic, deeply authentic.

2. Amalfi (Amalfi Coast)

  • Why visit: Iconic coastal town with pastel houses perched on cliffs.
  • Highlights: Amalfi Cathedral, scenic boat rides, and lemon groves.
  • Atmosphere: Romantic and picturesque.

3. Pompeii

  • Why visit: Ancient Roman city preserved by volcanic ash from Vesuvius.
  • Highlights: Forum, amphitheater, Villa of the Mysteries.
  • Atmosphere: Archaeological wonder, frozen in time.

4. Caserta

  • Why visit: Known for the Royal Palace of Caserta, often compared to Versailles.
  • Highlights: Baroque palace, gardens, fountains, and theater.
  • Atmosphere: Regal and grand.

5. Massa Lubrense (Punta Campanella)

  • Why visit: Coastal nature reserve with hiking trails and ruins.
  • Highlights: Ancient temples, panoramic views of Capri and the Sorrento Peninsula.
  • Atmosphere: Natural, adventurous.

6. Benevento

  • Why visit: Historic inland city with Roman and medieval heritage.
  • Highlights: Arch of Trajan, medieval churches, folklore traditions.
  • Atmosphere: Cultural and historic.

Gemini

The best cities and towns to visit in Campania, Italy , offer an incredible contrast between gritty, high-energy street life, royal opulence, and some of the most dramatically beautiful coastlines in the world. Whether you are looking for world-class food, ancient history, or romantic seaside views, Campania delivers Italy at maximum volume.

The region is best explored by grouping its top destinations into the energetic metropolitan centers, the picture-perfect resort hubs, and the culturally rich historic cities inland.

The Big Coastal Hubs & Resorts

Naples (Napoli): The unapologetic capital of the region. You don't just visit Naples—you experience it. It is a thrilling sensory overload of narrow alleys draped in laundry, chaotic scooters, and historic treasures. Come here to wander down Spaccanapoli, marvel at the Sansevero Chapel Museum, and eat authentic Neapolitan pizza where it was born. It also serves as the ultimate train and ferry transit base for the entire region.

Sorrento: Perched safely on dramatic cliffs overlooking the Bay of Naples, Sorrento is the polished, easy-to-navigate alternative to Naples. It is heavily tailored to English-speaking travelers, famously filled with the scent of citrus groves, and serves as an incredibly well-connected jumping-off point to catch ferries to Capri or the local Circumvesuviana train to Pompeii.

Salerno: Located at the eastern end of the Amalfi Coast, Salerno is a thriving, highly liveable port city that many tourists unfairly sleep on. It features a gorgeous medieval old town, excellent dining at much lower prices than the nearby tourist traps, and a lively seaside promenade (Lungomare). It's the perfect budget-friendly base for exploring.

The Iconic Cliffside Gems

Positano: The poster child of the Amalfi Coast. Its pastel-colored houses cascade down a near-vertical cliff directly into the Tyrrhenian Sea. It is unashamedly glamorous, romantic, and crowded—be prepared to climb hundreds of steps and pay a premium for the views.

Amalfi: Once a powerful maritime republic, Amalfi offers a beautiful blend of history and coastal charm. The centerpiece is its striking, Moorish-influenced Cathedral of St. Andrew standing proudly above the main piazza. It has excellent ferry connections to surrounding towns and islands.

Ravello: High in the hills above the sea, Ravello completely bypasses the beach crowd for a vibe of absolute serenity and refined luxury. It is world-renowned for its historic estates, lush cliffside gardens like Villa Cimbrone, and its open-air summer music festivals.

The Historic Inland Cities

Caserta: Located just north of Naples, people visit Caserta for one massive reason: the breathtaking Royal Palace of Caserta. Often called the "Versailles of Italy," this gargantuan UNESCO World Heritage site boasts over 1,200 rooms and miles of fountain-lined English gardens.

Benevento: Deep in Campania's rugged interior, Benevento is a dream for history buffs who want to escape the coastal crowds. It features an exceptionally well-preserved Roman heritage, including the magnificent Arch of Trajan, an ancient theater, and a mystical connection to local folklore and witch legends.

Grok · grok-3

Naples (Napoli) is the essential starting point and the region’s vibrant capital. It has Europe’s largest historic center (a UNESCO site), intense street life, world-famous pizza and street food, Baroque churches, underground catacombs, the National Archaeological Museum, and views of Vesuvius and the Bay of Naples. It’s gritty, energetic, and full of character.

Amalfi Coast towns (especially Positano, Amalfi, and Ravello) are the postcard-perfect highlights. These cliffside villages feature dramatic scenery, pastel houses, beaches, and exclusive vibes. Amalfi has a historic cathedral and maritime history; Positano is the most photogenic and glamorous; Ravello sits higher up with gardens and quieter luxury. They’re crowded in peak season (May–October) but magical. Nearby Sorrento serves as a convenient, lively base on the peninsula with good ferry links.

Pompeii (and nearby Herculaneum/Ercolano) are must-visit archaeological sites rather than typical modern cities. Pompeii is the vast, world-famous Roman town frozen by the 79 CE eruption of Vesuvius. Herculaneum is smaller, better preserved in places, and less crowded. Both offer an unforgettable look at daily Roman life.

Capri (and the nearby islands of Ischia and Procida) stand out as glamorous and scenic day trips or longer stays from Naples. Capri is the glamorous “supermodel” island with the Blue Grotto, dramatic cliffs, and high-end shopping. Ischia offers thermal spas, gardens, and a more relaxed feel. Procida is the quieter, pastel-hued, authentic alternative.

Salerno is a practical and underrated gateway to the Amalfi Coast. It has a lively historic center, an impressive cathedral, a long promenade, and a more local, less touristy atmosphere than the Amalfi villages themselves.

Caserta is worth a dedicated visit for the enormous Royal Palace of Caserta (Reggia di Caserta), a UNESCO site often called the “Versailles of Naples,” with vast gardens. The modern town itself is less compelling, but the palace is a major draw.

Paestum (in the Cilento area) features three of Europe’s best-preserved ancient Greek temples in a spacious archaeological park, plus nearby beaches. It’s less crowded than Pompeii and pairs well with the wilder, more affordable Cilento Coast towns (such as Agropoli or Castellabate) if you want nature and authenticity beyond the Amalfi crowds.

Quick tips

  • For a first visit: Focus on Naples + Pompeii/Herculaneum + Amalfi Coast (or Capri) for a classic mix of city, history, and scenery.
  • Time needed: A long weekend works for Naples and nearby sites; a week+ lets you add the coast and islands properly.
  • Crowds & cost: Amalfi Coast and Capri are expensive and busy in summer; shoulder seasons or the Cilento area are more relaxed and affordable.
  • Other mentions: Inland spots like Benevento (Roman and medieval monuments) or the Phlegraean Fields near Naples appeal to history and nature fans, but the coastal and archaeological highlights dominate most recommendations.

These destinations consistently top travel guides (Lonely Planet, National Geographic, etc.) for their combination of history, food, scenery, and culture. Prioritize based on your interests—food and energy in Naples, beaches and views on the coast, or ancient ruins inland.
