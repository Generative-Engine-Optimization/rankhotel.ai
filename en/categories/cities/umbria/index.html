Umbria is fantastic for a trip built around hill towns, medieval architecture, art, food, and countryside. The region is compact enough that you can combine several towns without spending your whole trip driving.

The places I'd put at the top of your list

  1. Assisi - the essential visit

    • Famous for the Basilica of St. Francis and its extraordinary frescoes.
    • Beautiful medieval streets climbing the hillside.
    • Great views over the Umbrian valley.
    • Time: ½-1 day
      Assisi, Perugia and Orvieto are consistently identified among Umbria's most visited destinations.

  2. Orvieto - spectacular setting

    • Perched dramatically on a volcanic tufa plateau.
    • Its cathedral is one of Italy's great Gothic buildings.
    • Wonderful underground tunnels and Etruscan history.
    • Excellent place for food and wine.
    • Time: 1 day

  3. Perugia - best for a proper city

    • Umbria's regional capital and largest cultural center.
    • Historic center, museums, medieval streets and lively university atmosphere.
    • Time: 1 day
    • A good base if you don't want to change hotels constantly.

  4. Spoleto - art + medieval atmosphere

    • One of my favorite choices for combining history with a more relaxed feel.
    • Roman and medieval architecture, the Rocca fortress and spectacular Ponte delle Torri.
    • Also a good base for exploring southern Umbria.
    • Time: ½-1 day

  5. Gubbio - medieval Umbria

    • One of the region's most atmospheric medieval towns.
    • Dramatic stone buildings climbing the lower slopes of Mount Ingino.
    • Particularly good if you like medieval architecture and quieter towns.
    • Time: ½-1 day

Smaller towns that are absolutely worth adding

  • Spello - tiny, gorgeous, flower-filled streets and Roman remains. Pair it with Assisi.
  • Montefalco - excellent for wine, especially Sagrantino, plus sweeping countryside views.
  • Todi - elegant hill town with a beautiful central piazza.
  • Bevagna - flatter and more intimate, with a lovely medieval center.
  • Norcia - particularly interesting for food and as a gateway to the mountains.

These smaller towns are particularly easy to combine into day trips; a current Umbria itinerary from the region's Chamber of Commerce specifically highlights Todi, Spoleto, Trevi, Bevagna, Spello and Montefalco as worthwhile stops.

If I were planning a first trip

4 days:
Perugia → Assisi + Spello → Orvieto → Spoleto

5-7 days:
Perugia → Gubbio → Assisi + Spello → Montefalco/Bevagna → Spoleto → Orvieto

If you have a car, I'd strongly favor the second approach because the smaller hill towns and countryside are a big part of what makes Umbria special, rather than just visiting the largest cities.

Gemini

The best cities and towns to visit in Umbria are Perugia , Assisi , Orvieto , Spoleto , Gubbio , and Spello . Often called the "green heart of Italy," this region offers a quieter, more medieval alternative to neighboring Tuscany, balancing world-class art and architecture with deep culinary roots.

If you are looking for the absolute quintessential hub, Perugia is my top choice because it effortlessly pairs dense Etruscan and medieval history with the lively, modern energy of a historic university city.

The Top Cities & Towns to Explore

Perugia   The region's capital and transportation hub . It features an incredible historic center reached by a network of escalators cutting through ancient fortress walls. Walk down Corso Vannucci to see the Palazzo dei Priori and the magnificent Fontana Maggiore. Insider Tip: Perugia is world-famous for its chocolate. Visit during October to experience the massive Eurochocolate Festival.

Assisi   A breathtaking UNESCO World Heritage site built from unique local pink limestone that seems to glow at sunset. As the birthplace of St. Francis, its crown jewel is the split-level Basilica of San Francesco , which houses revolutionary medieval frescoes by Giotto and Cimabue. Insider Tip: It is the most visited town in Umbria. To beat the tour buses, explore its steep, quiet side alleys early in the morning or stay overnight to enjoy the peaceful, illuminated piazzas after the day-trippers leave.

Orvieto   Perched dramatically on a volcanic tufa cliff , Orvieto is famous for its jaw-dropping Gothic Duomo, which boasts a dazzling, golden mosaic facade. Beyond the church, you can descend into Orvieto Underground, a labyrinth of Etruscan-era tunnels and olive presses carved straight into the rock cliff. Insider Tip: Try a glass of Orvieto Classico, a crisp, local white wine, paired with Umbrian wild boar ragù at a local trattoria.

Spoleto   A gorgeous foothill city bridging the gap between urban culture and pristine nature. Spoleto spans Roman ruins, a striking 14th-century fortress (Rocca Albornoziana), and the towering Ponte delle Torri , a massive medieval aqueduct stretching across a deep gorge. Insider Tip: Spoleto is incredibly walkable and features a modern underground system of travelators to help you navigate its steep hills without exhausting your legs.

Gubbio   One of Umbria's oldest and most wonderfully preserved medieval towns, built right into the steep slopes of Mount Ingino. It has an austere, fairy-tale stone appearance dominated by the massive Piazza Grande, a "suspended" public square built out over the hillside. Insider Tip: Take the Funivia Colle Eletto—a unique, open-air "birdcage" cable car—up to the Basilica of Sant'Ubaldo at the top of the mountain for panoramic valley views.

Spello   The ultimate "hidden gem" village for travelers who just want to wander. Spello is famous for its incredibly narrow, winding stone alleyways that residents meticulously decorate with cascading flower pots and vibrant blooms year-round. Insider Tip: Don't miss the Villa dei Mosaici, an exceptional, climate-controlled museum built directly over the ruins of a Roman villa discovered with its vibrant mosaic floors completely intact.

Choosing a Travel Base

If traveling by train: Stay in Perugia . It operates as the region's primary transport hub, making it easy to catch regional trains directly to Assisi, Spoleto, or Lake Trasimeno without needing a car.

If you have a rental car: Consider boutique towns like Spello or Bevagna . They offer central geographic locations, easier parking, and a peaceful environment to return to after a day of driving through the countryside.

Grok · grok-3

Assisi, Perugia, Orvieto, Spoleto, and Gubbio stand out as the top cities/towns to visit in Umbria (Italy’s “green heart”), with several charming smaller spots close behind. Umbria is known for well-preserved medieval hill towns, art, religious history, excellent food and wine, and far fewer crowds than neighboring Tuscany.

Top Recommendations

Here’s a breakdown of the strongest picks based on consistent travel recommendations:

  • Assisi — The must-visit highlight for most people. Birthplace of St. Francis (and St. Clare), it features the UNESCO-listed Basilica of San Francesco with famous frescoes by Giotto and Cimabue. The pink-and-white stone town cascades down Monte Subasio with atmospheric streets, the Temple of Minerva, and sweeping views. It draws pilgrims but remains deeply atmospheric, especially early or late in the day.

  • Perugia — Umbria’s lively capital and a true city (university town energy). The historic center has winding medieval streets, the impressive Palazzo dei Priori (home to the Galleria Nazionale dell’Umbria), the Fontana Maggiore, Etruscan walls, and underground passages. It’s also famous for chocolate (Perugina Baci and the annual Eurochocolate festival). Great as a base or for evening passeggiata.

  • Orvieto — Dramatically perched on a volcanic cliff (tufa rock). Its Gothic Duomo is one of Italy’s most spectacular cathedrals, with an ornate façade and rich interiors. Explore the underground labyrinth of Etruscan tunnels and caves, stroll the side streets for ceramics and local food (try dishes with Orvieto wine), and enjoy the elevated views.

  • Spoleto — Many people’s personal favorite for its blend of Roman ruins, Lombard and medieval architecture, a beautiful cathedral (with frescoes by Filippo Lippi), the dramatic Ponte delle Torri aqueduct/bridge, and wooded surroundings. It hosts the renowned Festival dei Due Mondi (arts festival) in summer. Charming year-round without feeling overwhelming.

  • Gubbio — A classic austere medieval hill town (“the gray city”) of stone buildings clinging to a mountainside. Highlights include the dramatic hanging Piazza Grande, Palazzo dei Consoli, and a cable car up Monte Ingino. It feels very authentic and gives a strong sense of medieval Italy.

Excellent Smaller Towns & Villages

These are often equally rewarding and less crowded:

  • Spello — Flower-filled alleys, Roman mosaics, and Pinturicchio frescoes. Especially magical during the Infiorata (flower carpet festival, usually late spring/early summer).
  • Todi — Elegant hilltop town with outstanding views, a handsome main square, and ancient Roman cisterns beneath it.
  • Montefalco — Nicknamed the “Balcony of Umbria” for its views; famous for Sagrantino red wine and a museum with Benozzo Gozzoli frescoes.
  • Bevagna — Flatter and more intimate, with lovely Romanesque churches and a strong sense of place (home to a medieval-themed market festival).

Quick Tips

  • Base yourself in Perugia, Spoleto, or a countryside agriturismo for easy day trips by car (highly recommended, as public transport is limited between smaller towns). Assisi and Orvieto also work well as bases.
  • Best time: Spring (April–June) and fall (September–October) for mild weather, fewer crowds, and festivals. Summer can be hot and busier in the main spots.
  • Umbria rewards slow travel—wandering streets, local wine (Sagrantino, Orvieto Classico), truffles, olive oil, cured meats, and chocolate.

These places capture the region’s essence of art-filled hill towns, spiritual heritage, and unspoiled countryside. Prioritize Assisi + Orvieto + one or two others if time is short.
