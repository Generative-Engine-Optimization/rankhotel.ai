If it’s your first trip to Tuscany, I’d focus on a mix of major art cities and smaller medieval towns. The Tuscan tourism board itself highlights Florence, Pisa, Siena, San Gimignano, Lucca, and Arezzo among its key destinations.

My shortlist

  • Florence - Best for art, history & Renaissance architecture.
    The essential first stop: the Duomo, Uffizi, Ponte Vecchio, Palazzo Pitti, Michelangelo, Botticelli, and Medici history. You could easily spend 2-3 days here.

  • Siena - Best for medieval atmosphere.
    Much smaller and more intimate than Florence, with Piazza del Campo, the cathedral, winding streets, and the surrounding Tuscan countryside.

  • Lucca - Best for a relaxed, romantic city.
    Its exceptionally well-preserved Renaissance walls surround a compact historic center with Piazza dell'Anfiteatro, churches, towers, cafés, and pleasant streets for wandering.

  • San Gimignano - Best for the classic Tuscan postcard.
    Famous for its medieval towers and hilltop setting among vineyards. It's relatively small, so it's particularly good as a half-day or day trip.

  • Pisa - Best for iconic sights.
    The Leaning Tower and Piazza dei Miracoli are the obvious draws, but the city also has attractive riverfronts and historic streets.

  • Arezzo - Best for something a little less touristy.
    A historic hill city with Piazza Grande, medieval architecture, Roman/Etruscan heritage, and important Renaissance art including Piero della Francesca.

If I were planning a 7-day trip

Florence (3 nights) → Siena (2 nights) → Lucca (1 night) → San Gimignano (day trip)

And I'd dedicate some time to the Val d'Orcia/Chianti countryside, because Tuscany isn't just about its cities-the rolling hills, vineyards, olive groves, and small villages are a major part of the experience.

The best cities and towns to visit in Tuscany depend entirely on what you want out of your trip—whether that is world-class Renaissance art, dramatic medieval architecture, or legendary wine tasting. To give you the perfect snapshot of the region, the absolute top destinations include Florence , Siena , Lucca , San Gimignano , and Montepulciano .

The Big Two: Art & Medieval Majesty

Florence: The undisputed jewel of Tuscany and the birthplace of the Renaissance . It is best for travelers who want world-famous museums like the Uffizi, historic architecture like Brunelleschi's dome, and vibrant dining.

Siena: A breathtaking red-brick medieval city built across three hills. It centers around the striking Piazza del Campo (home to the famous Palio horse race) and features a stunning black-and-white striped Duomo.

The Charming Wall & Tower Towns

Lucca: A charming, flat, and deeply relaxing city surrounded by a perfectly intact Renaissance wall . The top of the wall has been converted into a panoramic park where you can rent a bicycle to ride around the entire perimeter of the old town. Users on Reddit widely praise Lucca as a fantastic, less hectic base for day trips across Tuscany.

San Gimignano: Famous for its iconic skyline of 14 medieval stone towers rising above the hills. It offers spectacular valley views, though it is highly popular with day-trippers, so it is best experienced in the quiet evening hours.

The Wine & Countryside Hilltops

Montepulciano: A rugged, steep hill town crowning a limestone ridge in southern Tuscany. It serves as a dream destination for wine lovers, famous for its Vino Nobile di Montepulciano and underground historic wine cellars.

Pienza: Nestled right in the picture-perfect Val d'Orcia valley, this tiny UNESCO World Heritage site was redesigned in the 1400s to be the "ideal Renaissance city". Go here for incredible valley views, romantic cobblestone alleys, and legendary Pecorino cheese.

Grok · grok-3

Florence, Siena, Pisa, Lucca, and San Gimignano consistently rank as the top cities and towns to visit in Tuscany, with excellent supporting options in the hill towns and wine regions.

Tuscany blends major Renaissance and medieval cities with smaller hilltop towns set amid rolling countryside, vineyards, and cypress-lined roads. Most first-time visitors prioritize Florence + Siena (or one other major city) and add 1–3 hill towns or a Val d’Orcia/Chianti drive. Here’s a practical ranking of the standouts:

Essential Cities

  • Florence (Firenze) — The capital and cultural heart of Tuscany (and a cradle of the Renaissance). Must-sees include the Duomo and Brunelleschi’s dome, Uffizi Gallery, Accademia (Michelangelo’s David), Ponte Vecchio, and the historic center (UNESCO). It’s lively, art-packed, and the best transport hub, but also the busiest and most touristy. Book major museum tickets well ahead.
  • Siena — One of Italy’s best-preserved medieval cities and Florence’s historic rival. The shell-shaped Piazza del Campo (site of the famous Palio horse race), the striking Gothic Duomo, and the atmospheric car-free historic center make it magical—especially in the evening after day-trippers leave. Excellent base for the surrounding countryside.
  • Pisa — Famous for the Leaning Tower, but the whole Campo dei Miracoli (Square of Miracles—cathedral, baptistery, and tower) is a UNESCO World Heritage site worth seeing. The old town along the Arno is pleasant and less crowded than the tower area. Easy day trip from Florence or Lucca.
  • Lucca — A charming, relaxed walled city with intact Renaissance ramparts you can walk or bike along. Highlights include the oval Piazza dell’Anfiteatro (built on a Roman amphitheater), Guinigi Tower (with trees on top), and elegant streets full of shops and cafés. Often praised as less touristy than Florence or Siena while still full of character.

Outstanding Hill Towns & Smaller Cities

These deliver the classic “postcard Tuscany” feel and pair well with countryside drives:

  • San Gimignano — The “Medieval Manhattan,” famous for its surviving stone towers, panoramic views, frescoed churches, and Vernaccia wine. Very popular by day; much more atmospheric in the evening.
  • Montepulciano — Dramatic hilltop town with excellent Vino Nobile wine, elegant architecture, and sweeping views. Great for wine tastings and a slower pace.
  • Pienza — The “ideal Renaissance town” in the UNESCO-listed Val d’Orcia, known for perfect town planning, pecorino cheese, and beautiful surrounding landscapes.
  • Volterra — Quieter and more authentic, with strong Etruscan roots, a Roman theater, medieval walls, and alabaster workshops. Feels less polished and touristy.
  • Arezzo — Underrated larger town with Piero della Francesca frescoes, a fine main square, and one of Italy’s best monthly antiques fairs. Good for art and a less-crowded experience.
  • Cortona — Hill town with panoramic views, Etruscan history, and the Under the Tuscan Sun association. Attractive and atmospheric.

Wine-focused options worth adding: Montalcino (Brunello) and the broader Chianti region (Greve and surrounding villages).

Quick Planning Tips

  • First visit (5–7 days): Base in Florence (or split with Siena), day-trip to Pisa/Lucca or San Gimignano, and do a Val d’Orcia or Chianti drive for scenery and wine.
  • With a car: The smaller hill towns and countryside open up dramatically; public transport works well for Florence–Siena–Pisa–Lucca but is limited elsewhere.
  • Crowds & timing: Peak summer is busiest (especially Florence and San Gimignano). Spring and fall offer better weather, fewer people, and beautiful landscapes. Book Florence attractions early.
  • Beyond the cities: The real magic of Tuscany often lies in the countryside, thermal springs, and slow meals with local wine and food.

These destinations cover the classic highlights that appear across recent travel guides. Prioritize based on your interests (Renaissance art → Florence; medieval atmosphere → Siena; relaxed walls & bikes → Lucca; towers & views → San Gimignano; wine & hills → Montepulciano/Pienza). Enjoy the trip!

Perplexity

The best cities to visit in Tuscany depend on your interests, but a core shortlist for most travelers includes Florence, Siena, Lucca, Pisa, San Gimignano, Montepulciano, Pienza, Volterra, Arezzo, and Cortona.

The essentials (first-time visitors)

These are the most frequently recommended bases and day-trip hubs because they combine art, history, good transport, and iconic scenery.

  • Florence – The Renaissance capital and usual starting point. Highlights: Duomo, Uffizi, Accademia (David), Ponte Vecchio, and excellent food and shopping. Best for: art, museums, and rail-connected day trips across Tuscany.
  • Siena – Medieval rival to Florence, built around the shell-shaped Piazza del Campo and famous for the Palio horse race. Best for: Gothic architecture, atmospheric streets, and southern Tuscany access.
  • Lucca – A walled city with intact Renaissance ramparts you can cycle atop, plus charming churches and piazzas. Best for: relaxed strolling, cycling the walls, and a less-crowded alternative to Florence.
  • Pisa – Known for the Leaning Tower and cathedral complex in the Piazza dei Miracoli; also has a lively university vibe. Best for: a half-day to full-day stop, often combined with Lucca.
  • San Gimignano – The “Medieval Manhattan” of Tuscany, famous for its skyline of towers. Best for: photos, gelato, and easy Chianti/Val d’Orcia itineraries.

Wine, views, and picture-perfect towns

These smaller cities and hill towns are ideal if you want classic Tuscan landscapes, wine tasting, and slower pacing.

  • Montepulciano – Perched hill town renowned for Vino Nobile wines, cellars carved into tufa, and panoramic views. Best for: wine lovers and sunset vistas.
  • Pienza – Tiny Renaissance “ideal city” in the Val d’Orcia, famous for pecorino cheese and sweeping countryside views. Best for: photography, cheese tasting, and Val d’Orcia road trips.
  • Montalcino – Home of Brunello di Montalcino, with a fortress and serious wine culture. Best for: premium red-wine tastings and rustic dining.
  • Cortona – Etruscan hill town with narrow lanes, museums, and sweeping views over the Valdichiana. Best for: a quieter, atmospheric base with good restaurants.
  • Volterra – Ancient Etruscan city with alabaster craftsmanship, a Roman theatre, and dramatic clifftop setting. Best for: history buffs and off-the-beaten-path vibes.

Art, history, and alternatives to Florence

If you want culture without Florence-level crowds, or you’re returning to Tuscany, these cities add depth.

  • Arezzo – Rich in frescoes (notably Piero della Francesca), antiques markets, and a lived-in Tuscan feel. Best for: art beyond the big-name museums and a more local atmosphere.
  • Monteriggioni, Massa Marittima, Anghiari, Sorano, Sovana – Smaller medieval gems often recommended for travelers seeking quiet lanes, fortresses, and fewer tourists.

How to choose based on your trip style

  • First visit, no car: Base in Florence (or Siena) and day-trip to Pisa/Lucca, San Gimignano, and a Chianti/Val d’Orcia tour.
  • Romantic / scenic road trip: Stay in the Val d’Orcia (near Pienza/Montalcino) and tour Pienza, Montepulciano, Montalcino, plus Siena.
  • Art-focused: Florence plus Arezzo, with optional stops in Pienza and Volterra for Renaissance and Etruscan layers.
  • Medieval towns & photography: Siena, San Gimignano, Volterra, Montepulciano, Pienza, and lesser-known Monteriggioni or Massa Marittima.
