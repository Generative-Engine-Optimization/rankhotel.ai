«what are the best cities to visit in Emilia-Romagna?»

ChatGPT · gpt-5-6

Emilia-Romagna is one of Italy’s best regions for a city-hopping trip: you get medieval and Renaissance centers, incredible food, mosaics, cars, and the Adriatic coast. The main cities are also relatively easy to connect by train.

If I were planning a first trip, I’d focus on these:

City Best for How long
Bologna Food, atmosphere, architecture 2-3 days
Ravenna Byzantine mosaics & history 1 day
Modena Food & Ferrari/motoring 1 day
Parma Parmesan, prosciutto & elegant city center 1-2 days
Ferrara Renaissance architecture & cycling 1 day
Rimini Beach + Roman history + nightlife 1-2 days

1. Bologna - the essential first stop

Bologna is probably the best base for exploring the region. Its historic center has arcades, medieval towers and Piazza Maggiore, while the city's food culture is a major attraction. The famous porticoes are also UNESCO-listed.

Don't miss:

  • Piazza Maggiore
  • the medieval towers
  • the Quadrilatero food market area
  • the porticoes toward San Luca
  • tagliatelle al ragù, tortellini and mortadella

Piazza Maggiore is a good starting point for exploring the center.

2. Ravenna - spectacular mosaics

Ravenna is the standout if you love art and history. Its eight early-Christian and Byzantine monuments are UNESCO World Heritage sites, famous for extraordinarily preserved mosaics.

The highlights include Basilica of San Vitale and Mausoleo di Galla Placidia.

My suggestion: give Ravenna a full day rather than trying to squeeze it into a few hours.

3. Modena - food + Ferrari

Modena is compact but exceptionally rewarding. It's associated with traditional balsamic vinegar, excellent food and Ferrari, while its cathedral complex is one of the city's architectural highlights.

See:

  • Piazza Grande
  • Modena Cathedral
  • Ghirlandina tower
  • a traditional balsamic producer
  • the Ferrari museum if you're interested in cars

Piazza Grande and Modena Cathedral are right in the historic core.

4. Parma - perhaps the best food-focused stop

Parma combines a beautiful historic center with some of Italy's most famous foods: Parmigiano Reggiano and Prosciutto di Parma. The regional tourism authority specifically highlights Parma alongside Modena and Reggio Emilia for Emilia-Romagna's gastronomic heritage.

I'd visit the cathedral and Palazzo della Pilotta, then devote part of the trip to food.

5. Ferrara - Renaissance Italy at a slower pace

Ferrara has a very different feel: quieter, spacious and exceptionally good for walking or cycling. Its historic center and Este heritage are UNESCO-listed.

The centerpiece is Castello Estense. The city walls are also worth exploring.

Best choice if: you want a beautiful, relatively relaxed day away from the busier tourist circuit.

6. Rimini - for the coast

Rimini is best known for its Adriatic beaches and nightlife, but there's more to it than seaside tourism. The historic center contains Roman monuments including the Arch of Augustus and Tiberius Bridge.

Arco di Augusto is an easy place to begin exploring the old city.

If you only have 4-5 days

I'd do:

Bologna → Modena → Parma → Ravenna → Ferrara

Bologna works particularly well as the base because the main cities are connected along the regional rail corridor.

If you have 7+ days, add Rimini and perhaps some smaller places such as Brisighella, Dozza or Comacchio. The region's official tourism site highlights these smaller towns for their distinctive historic character.

For a first visit, my practical shortlist would be Bologna, Ravenna, Modena and Parma. They give you four very different sides of Emilia-Romagna: city life, Byzantine art, automotive culture and food.

🏰 Notable Attractions

  • Bologna: The National Art Gallery and MAMbo Museum of Modern Art showcase centuries of Italian creativity.
  • Parma: The Torrechiara Castle nearby offers breathtaking frescoes and countryside views.
  • Ravenna: The Basilica di San Vitale and Mausoleum of Galla Placidia are unmatched for mosaics.
  • Ferrara: The Este Castle and intact city walls make it feel like stepping back into the Renaissance.

🍝 Food & Culture

  • Emilia-Romagna is considered Italy’s food capital. Bologna is home to tagliatelle al ragù (often miscalled “spaghetti bolognese”), Parma to prosciutto and cheese, and Modena to traditional balsamic vinegar.
  • Each city also hosts vibrant festivals, from Parma’s Verdi Festival to Bologna’s lively student scene.

⚠️ Things to Keep in Mind

  • Crowds: Bologna and Ravenna can be busy, especially in summer.
  • Costs: Parma and Modena’s gourmet experiences can be pricey, but worth it.
  • Transport: Trains connect all these cities easily, making day trips convenient.

If you’re planning a trip, I’d suggest starting in Bologna (central hub, great food, lively atmosphere), then branching out to Parma and Modena for culinary delights, and finishing in Ravenna and Ferrara for art and history.

Gemini

The best cities to visit in Emilia-Romagna represent a perfect crossroads of world-class gastronomy, Renaissance art, and cutting-edge automotive engineering . Because these major cities are linked directly by a single, straight train line (the ancient Roman road Via Emilia), they are incredibly easy to visit as day trips or as a multi-city tour.

The "Big Five" Must-Visit Cities

Bologna (The Vibrant Capital): Known as La Grassa (The Fat One) for its unbeatable food scene and La Dotta (The Learned One) for hosting Europe's oldest university. It serves as the logical base for your trip.Highlights: Strolling under miles of UNESCO-listed porticoes, people-watching in Piazza Maggiore, and tackling the historic climb up the Portico di San Luca.What to eat: Authentic tagliatelle al ragù, tortellini in brodo, and mortadella.

Ravenna (The Mosaic Masterpiece): A quiet, coastal city that holds a jaw-dropping collection of 8 UNESCO World Heritage sites . It boasts some of the most spectacularly preserved 5th- and 6th-century Byzantine mosaics in the Western world.Highlights: The Basilica of San Vitale, the Mausoleum of Galla Placidia, and Dante Alighieri’s tomb.

Parma (The Elegant Cultural Hub): Refined, walkable, and deeply artistic, Parma is famous for its music culture and high-end gastronomy.Highlights: The wooden masterpiece Teatro Farnese inside the sprawling Palazzo della Pilotta, and the stunning Romanesque Duomo.What to eat: Prosciutto di Parma and authentic Parmigiano Reggiano.

Modena (Fast Cars & Slow Food): Modena beautifully balances high-octane engineering with centuries-old culinary tradition.Highlights: The Enzo Ferrari Museum, climbing the Ghirlandina Tower, and booking a tasting at a local family-run acetaia.What to eat: True Aceto Balsamico Tradizionale di Modena (traditional balsamic vinegar aged for 12 to 25+ years).

Ferrara (The Renaissance Cycling City): A grand, fortress-walled city that was completely shaped by the powerful Este family. It has a peaceful, aristocratic energy and is famously flat, making it the bicycle capital of Italy.Highlights: The moated, medieval Castello Estense right in the city center.What to eat: Cappellacci di zucca (pumpkin-stuffed pasta served with ragù or sage butter).

Worthy Alternates & Hidden Gems

If you have a car or extra time, consider adding these unique destinations to your itinerary:

Rimini: If you want a break from art history, Rimini offers miles of sandy Adriatic beaches, Roman ruins (like the Tiberius Bridge), and a lively nightlife scene.

Dozza: A tiny hilltop village just outside Bologna famous for its painted walls , turning the entire medieval hamlet into a permanent, open-air modern art gallery.

Brisighella: A postcard-perfect medieval village nestled in the hills, crowned by three distinct structures: a fortress, a clocktower, and a sanctuary. It is world-renowned for its rare extra-virgin olive oil.

Grok · grok-3

Bologna, Ravenna, Parma, Modena, Ferrara, and Rimini stand out as the top cities to visit in Emilia-Romagna. The region is renowned for outstanding food (the “Food Valley”), medieval and Renaissance architecture, Byzantine art, and a mix of inland historic centers and Adriatic coast.

Top Cities

Bologna (the capital and main hub)
Italy’s food capital and home to the world’s oldest university. Highlights include the vast network of porticoes (UNESCO), Piazza Maggiore with the Neptune Fountain, the Two Towers (Asinelli), medieval streets, markets, and excellent local cuisine (tortellini, ragù, mortadella). It has a lively student atmosphere and makes an ideal base.

Ravenna
Famous for its extraordinary early Christian and Byzantine mosaics. Eight UNESCO World Heritage sites include the Basilica of San Vitale, Mausoleum of Galla Placidia, and others. Also visit Dante Alighieri’s tomb. It offers a quieter, more contemplative contrast to the bigger food cities.

Parma
Elegant and refined, known worldwide for Prosciutto di Parma and Parmigiano-Reggiano. See the Romanesque cathedral, the striking pink Baptistery, Teatro Regio, and the historic center. Excellent for food-focused visits and a more sophisticated vibe.

Modena
A polished smaller city with a UNESCO-listed Romanesque Duomo and Piazza Grande. It’s a center of the Motor Valley (Ferrari and Maserati museums nearby, plus Enzo Ferrari’s house), traditional balsamic vinegar production, and high-end dining (including Massimo Bottura’s Osteria Francescana). Also linked to Luciano Pavarotti.

Ferrara
A well-preserved Renaissance city shaped by the Este family. Key sights are the moated Castello Estense, the UNESCO historic center, wide streets ideal for walking or cycling, and the cathedral. It has a refined, less crowded atmosphere and local specialties like cappellacci pasta.

Rimini
The main Adriatic beach resort, with long sandy beaches and lively summer atmosphere/nightlife. It also has solid Roman heritage (Arch of Augustus, Tiberius Bridge) and connections to filmmaker Federico Fellini. Good for combining culture with coast time.

Other Notable Mentions

  • Reggio Emilia — Quieter, elegant, linked to Parmigiano-Reggiano and the Italian tricolor flag.
  • Piacenza — Western gateway city with a strong historic center.
  • Charming smaller towns and villages (often day-trip material) include Castell’Arquato, Brisighella, Dozza, and Comacchio.

Practical Tips

Most of the main cities are well connected by train along the Via Emilia corridor (Bologna is the central hub). Food is a major draw everywhere—expect outstanding cured meats, cheeses, fresh pasta, and local wines. The region rewards a mix of city-hopping and slower exploration of the surrounding countryside or coast.

For a short trip, prioritize Bologna + Modena/Parma + Ravenna. Add Ferrara or Rimini depending on whether you prefer Renaissance elegance or beach time.
