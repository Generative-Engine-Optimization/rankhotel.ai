«what are the best cities to visit in Piedmont?»

ChatGPT · gpt-5-6

If you mean Piedmont (Piemonte) in northern Italy, there’s a great mix of elegant cities, wine-country towns, lakes, and Alpine destinations. For a first trip, I’d focus on these:

  1. Turin (Torino) - the essential city

    • Grand Baroque architecture, elegant arcades, historic cafés and excellent museums.
    • Highlights include the Egyptian Museum, Mole Antonelliana, Royal Palace and Piazza Castello.
    • Allow 2-3 days. Turin is also Piedmont’s main tourism hub.

  2. Alba - best for food & wine

    • In the Langhe, surrounded by vineyards producing Barolo and Barbaresco.
    • Famous for white truffles, excellent restaurants and a charming historic center.
    • A particularly good base for exploring nearby wine villages. Alba was the most-visited destination in Cuneo province in 2025.

  3. Asti - medieval Piedmont + wine

    • Compact medieval center, towers, churches and atmospheric squares.
    • Great choice if you want Barbera, Asti Spumante and Monferrato without the scale of Turin.
    • The surrounding countryside is particularly attractive for driving or cycling.

  4. Verbania - best for lakes and gardens

    • On Lake Maggiore, with waterfront promenades, historic villas and gardens.
    • Excellent base for exploring the lake and the Borromean Islands.
    • Verbania had the second-highest number of tourist overnight stays in Piedmont in 2025, after Turin.

  5. Cuneo - underrated city + mountains

    • Elegant Piazza Galimberti, arcaded streets and a relaxed atmosphere.
    • Great gateway to the Alps and southern Piedmont, with excellent local food.
    • Combine it with the nearby Langhe if you have a car.

  6. Novara - for architecture and a quieter experience

    • Interesting historic center, Basilica of San Gaudenzio and its huge dome.
    • Convenient if you're traveling between Milan and the Piedmontese lakes.
    • Less touristy than Turin or the Langhe.

Also worth considering

  • Orta San Giulio - technically a small town rather than a city, but one of the most beautiful places in Piedmont, on Lake Orta.
  • Stresa - gorgeous Lake Maggiore resort and ideal for visiting the Borromean Islands.
  • Ivrea - interesting UNESCO-listed industrial heritage and famous Carnival.
  • Biella - good for combining a historic town with mountain scenery.

If I were planning a first Piedmont trip:
Turin → Alba/Langhe → Asti/Monferrato → Lake Maggiore (Stresa/Verbania) would give you a particularly varied introduction: city, food, wine, countryside and lakes.

✨ Travel Tips

  • Best time to visit: Spring and autumn for mild weather and wine festivals.
  • Food to try: White truffle dishes in Alba, bagna càuda (anchovy-garlic dip), and hazelnut desserts.
  • Getting around: Renting a car is the most efficient way to explore Piedmont’s scattered towns, vineyards, and mountain areas.

⚠️ Things to Keep in Mind

  • Crowds: Alba can be very busy during truffle season (October–November).
  • Costs: Turin offers luxury stays, but smaller towns like Cuneo and Novara are more budget-friendly.
  • Local authenticity: Piedmont is less touristy than Tuscany, so expect a more genuine Italian experience.

Gemini

Piedmont (Piemonte) is one of Italy's premier hidden gems, offering a sophisticated blend of royal history, world-class gastronomy, and alpine landscapes. Whether you are looking for majestic palaces, legendary wine tasting, or serene alpine lakes, here are the best cities and towns to experience in the region.

The Major Cultural Hubs

Turin (Torino)   The elegant capital of Piedmont, Turin is highly underrated and looks more like Paris than a typical Italian city due to its grand royal history. It features stunning Baroque architecture, vast grid-like porticoed streets perfect for strolling, and world-class museums like the Egyptian Museum and the Royal Palace of Turin. Best for: Architecture buffs, royal history, historic grand cafés, and city walking tours like the Free Walking Tour Turin.

Alba   Known internationally as the white truffle capital of the world, Alba is a lively, medieval town packed with gourmet boutique shops and historic red-brick towers. It acts as the gateway to the prestigious Langhe wine region. Best for: Foodies, truffle hunters, and wine enthusiasts looking for an ideal hub to explore the surrounding vineyards.

Asti   Famous across the globe for its sparkling white wine ( Asti Spumante ), Asti boasts one of the grandest Gothic cathedrals in the region. It provides a slightly quieter, authentic alternative to Alba and is packed with historic palaces and medieval charm. Best for: Sparkling wine tastings, historical street markets, and exploring the Monferrato hills.

Lakeside and Scenic Towns

Stresa   Perched beautifully on the shores of Lake Maggiore , Stresa is a majestic resort town defined by its Belle Époque lakeside grand hotels and floral promenades. It serves as the direct ferry launch point to the spectacular Borromean Islands, where you can tour lavish Baroque palaces. Best for: Lakeside relaxation, dramatic alpine backdrops, and day trips to island estates.

Orta San Giulio   Often voted one of the most romantic and prettiest towns in Italy , this completely pedestrianized medieval village hugs the quiet shores of Lake Orta. A quick boat ride away sits the magical, silent island of San Giulio, home to an active community of Benedictine nuns. Best for: Couples, quiet retreats, and escaping the heavier crowds of neighboring Lake Como.

Wine and Alpine Gateways

Barolo   Though small in size, the historic village of Barolo gives its name to Italy’s most prestigious red wine. The town is dominated by the medieval Castello Falletti , which houses a highly innovative museum dedicated entirely to the history and culture of winemaking. Best for: Serious wine collectors, vineyard cycling tours, and spectacular photography of rolling vineyard landscapes.

Cuneo   Known as the gateway to the Maritime Alps , Cuneo is an elegant city characterized by wide plazas and dramatic views of snowy peaks framing the horizon. It is highly appreciated for its lack of mass tourism, making it a peaceful, authentic base for alpine hikers. Best for: Outdoor enthusiasts, high-altitude hiking, and enjoying traditional mountain cuisine away from the crowds.

Turin (Torino) stands out as the top must-visit city, followed closely by wine and food hubs like Alba and Asti, lakeside spots such as Stresa and Orta San Giulio, and other charming provincial centers.

Piedmont (Piemonte) in northwest Italy mixes elegant urban culture, world-class wine and food (especially in the Langhe and Monferrato), royal history, Alpine scenery, and lakes. Here’s a practical rundown of the best cities and larger towns based on consistent recommendations from travel guides and local sources:

Top Cities & Towns

Rank / Priority City/Town Why Visit Highlights Suggested Time
1. Essential Turin (Torino) Elegant capital, underrated compared to other Italian cities; excellent base Mole Antonelliana (Cinema Museum), Egyptian Museum (one of the world’s best), Royal Palace & Savoy residences, long porticoed arcades, historic cafés (try bicerin), chocolate (gianduiotti) 2–3 days
2. Wine & Food Capital Alba Heart of the Langhe (UNESCO vineyard landscapes); truffle and wine hub Medieval towers & historic center, white truffle fair (autumn), Barolo/Barbaresco nearby, excellent restaurants 1–2 days (or base for the hills)
3. Historic & Sparkling Asti Medieval character + sparkling wine (Asti Spumante/Moscato) and the famous Palio horse race Cathedral, towers, historic center, Monferrato wine country access 1 day
4. Lakeside Elegance Stresa (Lake Maggiore) Classic resort town on the western (Piedmont) shore of Lake Maggiore Promenade, boat trips to Borromean Islands (Isola Bella, Isola Madre), nearby Mottarone views 1–2 days
5. Picturesque Lake Village Orta San Giulio (Lake Orta) Often called one of Italy’s prettiest lakeside spots Pedestrian historic center, Isola di San Giulio, Sacro Monte (UNESCO) 1 day (or overnight)
Other strong options Cuneo Gateway to southern Alps with a grand historic center Piazza Galimberti, long porticoes, nearby valleys 1–2 days
Saluzzo Medieval “forgotten” gem at the foot of the Alps Upper historic center, Castiglia fortress, nearby Castello della Manta 1 day
Novara Quiet provincial city with rice-country surroundings Basilica of San Gaudenzio (Antonelli cupola), Broletto, easy day trip from Milan/Turin Half–1 day
Biella Textile heritage + mountain access Medieval Piazzo district, Sanctuary of Oropa 1 day
Ivrea UNESCO industrial heritage + unique carnival Olivetti architecture, Battle of the Oranges (Carnival) 1 day

Quick Tips for Visiting

  • Best bases: Turin for culture and transport links; Alba for wine/truffles and the Langhe hills; Stresa or Orta for lakes; Saluzzo/Pinerolo or Cuneo for mountain access.
  • Wine focus: Prioritize Alba + nearby villages (Barolo, La Morra, Neive, Barbaresco). The Langhe-Roero-Monferrato landscapes are UNESCO-listed.
  • Getting around: Turin is well-connected by train. A car is highly recommended for the wine hills, smaller towns, and Alpine valleys.
  • Seasonal notes: Autumn (Sept–Nov) is peak for truffles, wine harvest, and foliage. Spring and early summer are great for milder weather and fewer crowds. Winter suits skiing in places like Sestriere.
  • Nearby day trips from Turin: Reggia di Venaria Reale (Savoy “Versailles”), Sacra di San Michele.

Turin + Alba/Langhe + one lake stop (Orta or Maggiore) covers the classic Piedmont highlights for most visitors. If you have more time or specific interests (skiing, hiking, rice fields, or quieter medieval towns), the secondary list above fills in nicely.
