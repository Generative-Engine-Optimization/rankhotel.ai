If you mean Rome, Italy, the city has an unusually strong luxury-hotel scene right now. Rather than one “best” hotel, I’d choose based on the kind of stay you want. Condé Nast Traveler’s 2026 guide highlights several of these properties among its top Rome picks.

My shortlist

  • Hotel de Russie, a Rocco Forte hotel - Excellent for a first trip: very central, near Piazza del Popolo and the Spanish Steps, with a beautiful garden. Condé Nast specifically names it a top choice for first-time visitors.
  • Six Senses Rome - Contemporary luxury right in the historic center, particularly appealing if you care about a great spa and modern design.
  • The St. Regis Rome - Classic grand-hotel experience, with elaborate interiors and strong service; Condé Nast lists it for its amenities.
  • Corinthia Rome - One of the newer luxury additions. It's a 60-room property in the former Bank of Italy offices, with restaurants, spa and terraces.
  • ROMEO Roma - Very high-end and design-forward, near the Piazza del Popolo area; particularly interesting if architecture/design matters to you.
  • Palazzo Manfredi - Particularly compelling if you want the Colosseum literally as part of the hotel experience.
  • InterContinental Rome Ambasciatori Palace by IHG - A polished option on Via Veneto; Condé Nast names it among its picks for first-time visitors.
  • Hotel NH Collection Roma Palazzo Cinquecento - Convenient if you're arriving/departing by train because it's right by Roma Termini.

If you want something especially special

I'd look closely at Six Senses Rome, Hotel de Russie, a Rocco Forte hotel, Corinthia Rome, and Palazzo Manfredi.

Rome’s top hotels (as of 2026) lean heavily toward luxury and design-forward properties, with consistent standouts across expert lists (Condé Nast, Time Out, Gourmet Traveller, The Times, Trip.com, U.S. News, and others) and guest ratings.

Here are some of the most frequently recommended:

Iconic Luxury Classics

  • Hassler Roma (Spanish Steps) — Family-run since the 19th century, perched at the top of the Spanish Steps. Michelin-starred Imàgo restaurant with panoramic views of the city and St. Peter’s. Consistently ranks among the world’s best for classic Roman glamour, service, and location.
  • Hotel de Russie (Rocco Forte, near Piazza del Popolo/Spanish Steps) — Famous for its terraced “Secret Garden” (one of Rome’s best hotel gardens), Stravinskij Bar, and refined elegance. A favorite of locals, celebrities, and first-time visitors seeking a serene yet central base.
  • Hotel Eden (Dorchester Collection, Via Veneto area) — Historic property with polished renovations, excellent rooftop dining/views, and classic luxury near shopping and parks.

Modern Luxury Standouts

  • Bvlgari Hotel Roma (Campo Marzio / Piazza Augusto Imperatore) — Often called one of the most complete and stylish luxury hotels in the city. Features a striking pool under mosaic vaults, Niko Romito restaurant, excellent suites, and a prime location near the Mausoleum of Augustus, Spanish Steps, and Pantheon. Frequently tops “best overall” or “most luxurious” lists.
  • Six Senses Rome (near Trevi Fountain / Via del Corso) — Wellness-focused in a historic palazzo with Patricia Urquiola design, Roman baths-inspired spa, and rooftop garden. Strong for design and relaxation.
  • Romeo Roma — Newer high-end opening noted for contemporary luxury, strong food offerings, and high guest scores.

Boutique & Design Favorites

  • J.K. Place Roma (near Pantheon) — Intimate, stylish townhouse with a private-club feel; frequently ranks very high for couples and design lovers.
  • Orient Express La Minerva (near Pantheon) — Newer arrival praised for historic location and elegant design.
  • Palazzo Manfredi — Small luxury property with exceptional Colosseum views (especially from the restaurant terrace). Ideal for a special occasion.

Other Strong Contenders

  • Anantara Palazzo Naiadi, Hotel de la Ville (Rocco Forte), St. Regis Rome, Portrait Roma, Villa Spalletti Trivelli, and newer openings like Corinthia Rome also appear regularly on expert lists.

Location tips:
Spanish Steps / Piazza del Popolo / Campo Marzio areas put you in the heart of shopping, restaurants, and major sights with easy walking. Near the Pantheon or Trevi is ultra-central but busier. Colosseum-area properties (like Palazzo Manfredi) offer dramatic views but are a bit farther from the historic center’s densest attractions.

Practical notes:
Prices for these top properties typically start in the high hundreds to well over €1,000–2,000+ per night (especially peak seasons April–June and September–October). Book early. Many offer excellent rooftop bars/restaurants that are worth visiting even if you’re not staying there.

The “best” depends on priorities—iconic views and old-world glamour (Hassler), garden serenity (de Russie), modern Italian luxury (Bvlgari), or wellness/design (Six Senses). For the absolute latest availability and rates, check Booking.com, the hotels’ sites, or a trusted luxury travel advisor, as Rome’s hotel scene continues to evolve with new openings.
