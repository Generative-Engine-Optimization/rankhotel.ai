Gemini

While Rome is the undisputed crown jewel and capital of the Lazio region, the surrounding territory is packed with incredible medieval hilltop towns, volcanic crater lakes, and coastal hideaways that remain largely untouched by international mass tourism. Depending on whether you are looking for deep Etruscan history, Renaissance gardens, or a relaxing seaside escape, the towns outside the capital offer an authentic glimpse into Italian life.

🏰 Historic Cities & Architectural Wonders

Rome   The Eternal City requires no introduction, serving as the cultural and historical epicenter of Italy. Walk through layers of history at the Colosseum and the Roman Forum, or marvel at Michelangelo's masterpieces inside the Vatican Museums. Concierge Tip: Don't limit yourself to the classic ruins; head to the vibrant Trastevere neighborhood in the evening to eat authentic Cacio e Pepe alongside locals.

Viterbo   Known as the City of the Popes , Viterbo boasts one of the most meticulously preserved medieval quarters in Europe (San Pellegrino). It served as the papal seat during the 13th century when Rome became too dangerous. Why visit: To escape the crowds and explore dark stone alleys, fountains, and the grand Palazzo dei Papi. Afterward, do what locals do and soak in the natural thermal hot springs just outside town.

Tivoli   Perched on the slopes of the Sabine Hills, Tivoli is an architectural paradise famous for its twin UNESCO World Heritage sites: the Renaissance water-gardens of Villa d'Este and the sprawling ancient ruins of Hadrian's Villa. Why visit: You can easily catch a direct train from Roma Termini. If you want a break from manicured gardens, hike through the rugged trails and deep gorges of the nearby Parco Villa Gregoriana to see Italy's second-highest waterfall.

🌊 Scenic Coastal Escapes

Sperlonga   A striking seaside town characterized by clusterings of whitewashed houses, narrow alleys, and pristine Blue Flag beaches. Emperor Tiberius loved this stretch of coast so much he built a massive vacation villa inside a local cave. Why visit: It is the ultimate summer retreat halfway between Rome and Naples. Spend your morning wandering the old town and your afternoon visiting the National Archaeological Museum of Sperlonga right on the water.

Gaeta   A historic, fortified harbor town built on a peninsula overlooking a dramatic gulf. Gaeta feels vibrant and entirely Italian, mostly skipped by international crowds because it lacks a direct train station. Why visit: For an incredible mix of military history and natural wonders. Hike up to the Montagna Spaccata (Split Mountain), a cliff split in half by a legendary fissure, which houses a sanctuary suspended over the sea. Be sure to sample Tiella di Gaeta, a savory local seafood pie.

🌋 Hilltop Villages & Volcanic Lakes

Civita di Bagnoregio   Dubbed The Dying City , this breathtaking medieval village sits on a crumbling pinnacle of volcanic tuff stone, rising lonely above a vast, rocky canyon. It is accessible exclusively via a steep pedestrian footbridge. Why visit: With fewer than twenty year-round residents, walking through its stone archways feels completely frozen in time. It provides an unforgettable, surreal landscape that looks like a fantasy novel.

Bracciano   Overlooking a massive, hyper-clean volcanic crater lake, Bracciano is dominated by the imposing 15th-century Castello Orsini-Odescalchi. Why visit: It is only 40 minutes north of Rome. You can tour the perfectly preserved Renaissance castle rooms, walk its battlements for sweeping lake views, and spend a relaxing afternoon renting umbrellas on the lakefront beach.

Castel Gandolfo   Nestled in the lush Castelli Romani hills south of Rome, this town sits on the rim of Lake Albano and is famously known as the traditional summer home of the Pope. Why visit: You can stroll through the elegant town center, tour the Pontifical Villas and gardens, and look down over the volcanic lake below. It is an easy day-trip base for hopped-hamlet exploring around neighboring wine towns like Frascati.

Grok · grok-3

Rome is the undisputed standout in Lazio (and Italy), but the region offers excellent variety beyond the capital: medieval hill towns, Etruscan sites, Renaissance villas, volcanic lakes, and attractive coastal spots.

Here are some of the best cities and towns to visit, based on frequent recommendations in travel guides:

Must-Visit Core Destinations

  • Rome — The Eternal City itself. Colosseum, Roman Forum, Pantheon, Vatican, piazzas, and world-class food and atmosphere. It’s the natural base for exploring the rest of Lazio.
  • Tivoli — Easy and highly rewarding day trip (about 40–60 minutes from Rome). Home to two UNESCO sites: the extravagant Renaissance gardens and fountains of Villa d’Este, and the sprawling ruins of Hadrian’s Villa (Villa Adriana).
  • Viterbo — Often called the “City of the Popes.” One of Italy’s best-preserved medieval centers (especially the San Pellegrino quarter), with the Palazzo dei Papi, cathedral, city walls, and nearby thermal baths. Roughly 1.5 hours north of Rome.

Dramatic Hill Towns & Cultural Gems

  • Civita di Bagnoregio — The iconic “dying city,” perched dramatically on a tufa outcrop above the Calanchi valley and reached only by a long pedestrian bridge. Extremely photogenic and atmospheric (best as a half-day or overnight visit).
  • Castelli Romani (especially Frascati and Castel Gandolfo) — Charming towns in the Alban Hills southeast of Rome. Frascati is famous for its wine and villas; Castel Gandolfo offers the Pope’s former summer palace and views over Lake Albano. Great for food, wine, and easy day trips.
  • Tarquinia — Outstanding for Etruscan heritage. UNESCO-listed painted tombs in the Monterozzi necropolis plus a strong archaeological museum, plus a pleasant historic center. About 1.5 hours northwest of Rome.

Coastal Highlights

  • Sperlonga — One of Lazio’s prettiest seaside towns: whitewashed houses stacked on a rocky promontory, good beaches, and the ruins of Tiberius’s villa with its museum. Ideal for 1–2 days.
  • Gaeta — Historic coastal city with a dramatic setting on Monte Orlando, medieval and Aragonese fortifications, beaches, and good seafood. A solid base for the southern Lazio coast.

Other Strong Contenders

  • Bracciano (and Lake Bracciano) — Lakeside town with the impressive Odescalchi Castle; good for a relaxed day by the water.
  • Subiaco — Mountain setting with important Benedictine monasteries (Sacro Speco and Santa Scolastica) and surrounding nature.
  • Caprarola — Home to the magnificent pentagonal Palazzo Farnese and its gardens.
  • Ostia Antica — Not a living city but Rome’s remarkably well-preserved ancient port (often compared to Pompeii with far fewer crowds); very close to the capital.

Quick Tips

  • Many of these (Tivoli, Castelli Romani, Bracciano, Ostia Antica) work well as day trips from Rome by train or car.
  • Northern Lazio (Viterbo, Civita di Bagnoregio, Tarquinia, Caprarola/Bomarzo area) rewards a longer stay or a multi-day loop.
  • Southern coast (Sperlonga, Gaeta) is ideal for combining history with beach time, especially spring through early autumn.
  • The region mixes ancient Roman, Etruscan, medieval papal, and Renaissance layers with volcanic landscapes and good local food/wine (Frascati whites, porchetta in the Castelli, etc.).

Rome + Tivoli + Viterbo (or Civita) makes an excellent first-time itinerary; add Sperlonga/Gaeta or the Castelli Romani depending on whether you prefer coast or countryside.

Perplexity

Rome is the standout, but Lazio has several splendid options besides it:

  • Viterbo (Medieval “City of the Popes,” well-preserved historic center)
  • Tivoli (Villa d’Este and Hadrian’s Villa)
  • Castel Gandolfo (Papal summer residence, views over Lake Albano)
  • Civita di Bagnoregio (dramatic stone village on a hill)
  • Lake Bracciano towns (Bracciano, Trevignano Romano) for lakeside scenery
  • Gaeta and Sperlonga along the coast for beaches and historic centers

If you want day trips from Rome, consider Ostia Antica for ancient ruins and Ariccia for food and views.
