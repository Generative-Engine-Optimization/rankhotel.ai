Lombardy is much more than Milan: you can combine major art cities, Alpine scenery, and the Italian lakes. If you're choosing where to spend your time, these are the places I'd put on your shortlist:

  1. Milan - best for a first trip
    The obvious starting point, with the Duomo, Brera, Sforza Castle, Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II, fashion/design, museums, and Leonardo's Last Supper.
    Milan

  2. Bergamo - best historic city
    Particularly beautiful is Città Alta, the medieval upper town enclosed by historic walls. It's compact, atmospheric, and makes an excellent 1-2 day trip.
    Bergamo

  3. Como - best for Lake Como
    Como itself is worth exploring for its cathedral, historic center and lakeside setting, but its bigger appeal is as a base for exploring the lake by boat, including places such as Bellagio, Varenna and Tremezzo.
    Como

  4. Brescia - best underrated choice
    A good option if you like history without the crowds of Milan or Lake Como. The city combines Roman remains, medieval architecture, elegant squares and access to Lake Garda, Lake Iseo and Franciacorta.
    Brescia

  5. Mantua (Mantova) - best for Renaissance art
    A gorgeous, relatively small art city surrounded by water, with the legacy of the Gonzaga family and attractions such as Palazzo Te. It's particularly good for architecture, art and a slower-paced visit.
    Mantua

  6. Cremona - best for a quieter authentic experience
    Famous for violin-making, with the impressive Torrazzo and cathedral dominating its historic center. It's a nice choice if you want somewhere less tourist-oriented.
    Cremona

  7. Pavia - best day trip from Milan
    A historic university city with attractive streets and churches, plus the spectacular Certosa di Pavia just outside the city.
    Pavia

If I were planning a Lombardy trip

I'd structure it roughly like this:

4-5 days: Milan → Bergamo → Como/Lake Como
7 days: Milan → Bergamo → Lake Como → Brescia → Mantua
10+ days: Add Pavia, Cremona, and some of the smaller Lake Como villages or the Valtellina.

One important distinction: Como is a city, while Lake Como is a whole destination. If your priority is scenery and charming villages rather than city sightseeing, I'd spend more time around the lake than in Como itself.

🌟 Why These Cities Stand Out

  • Milan is the gateway to Lombardy, blending modern skyscrapers with Gothic masterpieces.
  • Bergamo offers one of Italy’s most atmospheric medieval towns, perched on a hill with cobbled streets.
  • Brescia is less touristy but rich in Roman and Lombard heritage, perfect for history buffs.
  • Como is world-famous for its lake, villas, and proximity to Bellagio and Varenna.
  • Mantua is a jewel of Renaissance Italy, celebrated by artists like Mantegna.

⚠️ Travel Tips & Considerations

  • Crowds & Costs: Milan and Como can be expensive and crowded, especially in summer. Booking ahead is essential.
  • Hidden Gems: Cities like Cremona and Pavia are quieter alternatives with deep cultural roots.
  • Transport: Lombardy has excellent train connections — Milan is the hub, making day trips easy.
  • Best Season: Spring and autumn are ideal for mild weather and fewer tourists.

📌 Suggestion: If you want a balanced itinerary, spend 2–3 days in Milan, then add day trips to Bergamo, Como, and Mantua. For a deeper dive into history, include Brescia or Cremona.

Gemini

The absolute best cities to visit in Lombardy are Milan , Bergamo , and Mantua . While Milan anchors the region as a global powerhouse of fashion and art, Lombardy's true magic lies in its smaller provincial capitals. These cities offer magnificent Renaissance architecture, world-class culinary heritages, and far fewer crowds than nearby Venice or Florence.

The Heavy Hitters & Art Cities

Milan   The region's dynamic capital balances fast-paced modernity with staggering historical depth. Don't Miss: Take a walk among the spires on the roof of the spectacular Duomo di Milano and visit the grand Sforzesco Castle. Insider Tip: Skip the tourist traps directly outside the cathedral and head to the Navigli district in the evening for a canal-side aperitivo.

Bergamo   Split beautifully into two halves: the modern Città Bassa and the walled, hilltop Città Alta (Upper Town). Don't Miss: Wander the maze of medieval streets inside the UNESCO-protected Venetian walls. Insider Tip: Ride the historic funicular railway to get from the lower city to the upper ridge, and order casoncelli (a regional stuffed pasta) for dinner.

Mantua (Mantova)   Surrounded on three sides by artificial lakes, this Renaissance jewel feels like a floating palace complex. It was ruled by the wealthy Gonzaga family for centuries. Don't Miss: The staggering Palazzo Ducale and the mind-bending "Fall of the Giants" frescoes inside Palazzo Te. Insider Tip: Try the Tortelli di Zucca (pumpkin stuffer pasta), which is Mantua's signature culinary pride.

Cremona   The undisputed world capital of violin-making, famously home to master luthiers like Antonio Stradivari. Don't Miss: The Museo del Violino , where you can hear multimillion-dollar historic instruments played live in an acoustic auditorium. Insider Tip: Pick up some local torrone (nougat candy), which was invented here in the 15th century.

Lakeside & Historic Gems

Como   The main urban hub of the legendary Lake Como, combining dramatic Alpine views with a historic walled core. Don't Miss: The stunning Gothic-Renaissance Como Cathedral and the funicular up to the village of Brunate for sweeping views. Insider Tip: Como is historically famous for high-quality silk manufacturing; look for boutique silk shops tucked away in the pedestrian center.

Brescia   Often overlooked by tourists, Brescia boasts some of the most impressive and well-preserved Roman ruins in Northern Italy. Don't Miss: The Brixia archaeological park, which includes a Roman temple and a massive ancient amphitheater. Insider Tip: Brescia is the gateway to the Franciacorta wine region . Order a glass of Franciacorta DOCG—Italy’s answer to Champagne—at a local enoteca.

Pavia   A peaceful university town lined with red-brick medieval towers and cobblestone lanes. Don't Miss: The Certosa di Pavia , a breathtaking Carthusian monastery located just a short train or bike ride north of the city center. Insider Tip: Walk across the covered bridge (Ponte Coperto) spanning the Ticino River to explore the colorful Borgo Ticino neighborhood.

Grok · grok-3

Milan, Bergamo, Como, Brescia, Mantua (Mantova), Cremona, and Pavia consistently rank among the best cities (and major towns) to visit in Lombardy, Italy’s northern region of fashion, lakes, Renaissance heritage, and mountains.

Here’s a practical overview of the standouts:

Milan (Milano)

The regional capital and Italy’s fashion/finance hub. Highlights include the massive Gothic Duomo, Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II, Leonardo’s Last Supper, La Scala, the Navigli canals, and world-class shopping and food scenes. It’s the main transport hub and a vibrant base for day trips.

Bergamo

Often called one of the most beautiful. The Città Alta (Upper City) is a medieval hilltop gem enclosed by UNESCO-listed Venetian walls, with cobbled streets, piazzas, churches (Santa Maria Maggiore, Colleoni Chapel), and panoramic views. Reachable in under an hour from Milan by train; a funicular connects the lower and upper towns.

Como (and Lake Como towns)

Como itself sits at the southern end of the lake with a fine cathedral and funicular to Brunate for views. The real draw is the lake: elegant towns like Bellagio (classic “pearl of the lake” at the fork of the branches), Varenna, Tremezzo, and Menaggio offer villas, gardens, boat trips, and Alpine backdrops. Easily reached from Milan.

Brescia

The region’s second-largest city and often underrated. Strong Roman heritage (Capitolium temple and archaeological area), the excellent Santa Giulia Museum complex, elegant squares, and a lively atmosphere. It was Italian Capital of Culture (with Bergamo) in 2023.

Mantua (Mantova)

A Renaissance jewel almost completely surrounded by lakes, ruled for centuries by the Gonzaga family. Key sights include the vast Ducal Palace (with Mantegna frescoes) and Palazzo Te. Its historic center is a UNESCO World Heritage site and feels atmospheric and relatively uncrowded.

Cremona

Famous as the home of Stradivari and the violin-making tradition (visit the Violin Museum). The compact historic center features the Torrazzo (one of Italy’s tallest medieval towers, with a famous astronomical clock) and Romanesque architecture.

Pavia

A pleasant university city on the Ticino River with a well-preserved center, the covered Ponte Coperto, and the nearby magnificent Certosa di Pavia (Renaissance charterhouse). Close enough for an easy day trip from Milan.

Other strong options

  • Lecco — Scenic spot on Lake Como with mountain views and literary ties (Manzoni).
  • Varese — Gardens, villas, and the Sacro Monte; quieter and green.
  • Lakeside spots on Lake Garda (partly in Lombardy) such as Sirmione (castle and peninsula setting).
  • Further afield: Sondrio or Bormio for Valtellina Alps and outdoor activities.

Lombardy mixes urban energy, lake scenery, and historic centers very effectively. Most of these places are reachable by train from Milan in 30–120 minutes, making multi-stop itineraries straightforward. For a first visit, prioritize Milan + Bergamo or Lake Como, then add Mantua or Brescia depending on your interest in Renaissance art versus Roman history.
