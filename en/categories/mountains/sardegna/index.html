ChatGPT · gpt-5-6

If you’re looking for mountains, dramatic hiking, forests, gorges, and wild scenery rather than just beaches, Sardinia has several excellent areas. The big distinction is that its mountains are relatively low-the highest point is Punta La Marmora at 1,834 m-but the limestone terrain, cliffs and canyons make some areas feel much more rugged than the elevation suggests.

🏔️ 1. Supramonte - the wildest mountain landscape

Supramonte di Dorgali ⭐️ · 🗺️Direction·🌐Website·📞+390784927235

Where: Dorgali, Oliena, Orgosolo, Urzulei and Baunei
Best for: dramatic hiking, gorges, caves, cliffs and remote landscapes

This would be my first area to investigate if mountains are the priority. The Supramonte is a huge limestone massif with deep canyons, caves, sinkholes and almost roadless sections.

Highlights include:

  • Gola su Gorropu - enormous limestone canyon
  • Tiscali - prehistoric Nuragic settlement hidden inside the mountain
  • Punta Corrasi above Oliena
  • The mountains around Urzulei and Baunei
  • Trails descending toward the Gulf of Orosei

Gorropu Canyon is particularly spectacular, with canyon walls reaching several hundred metres.

⛰️ 2. Gennargentu - Sardinia's actual high mountains

Gennargentu National Park ⭐️ · 🗺️Direction·🌐Website·📞

Where: central Sardinia, around Fonni, Desulo, Aritzo and Tonara
Best for: summit hiking, mountain scenery, forests and cooler temperatures

This is the place to go if you specifically want the highest and most mountainous part of Sardinia. Punta La Marmora reaches 1,834 m, while Bruncu Spina is almost as high.

The landscape is quite different from Supramonte: more rounded mountain ridges, high pastures and forests rather than gigantic limestone walls.

Don't miss:

  • Punta La Marmora
  • Bruncu Spina
  • Desulo
  • Aritzo
  • The mountain villages of Barbagia

🧗 3. Supramonte di Baunei - mountains + sea

Supramonte di Urzulei ⭐️ · 🗺️Direction·🌐Website·📞

Where: Baunei/Urzulei on the east coast
Best for: spectacular scenery, challenging hikes, cliffs and beaches

This is probably the most visually spectacular combination of mountains and Mediterranean coastline. You get enormous limestone cliffs, gorges, caves and juniper-covered mountains dropping toward the Gulf of Orosei.

The famous hikes around Cala Goloritzè, Cala Luna and Cala Sisine are here.

If you want the feeling of "mountain wilderness directly above turquoise sea," I'd concentrate on this area.

🌲 4. Monte Arcosu - forests and wildlife

Riserva Naturale di Monte Arcosu Uta ⭐️ · 🗺️Direction·🌐Website·📞+390707968416

Where: southwest of Cagliari
Best for: quieter hiking, Mediterranean forest and wildlife

Monte Arcosu is less dramatic than Supramonte but is a good choice if you prefer forested mountain landscapes and wildlife rather than exposed limestone cliffs.

It's particularly interesting as a protected natural area and makes a convenient mountain excursion from the Cagliari area.

🌳 5. Monte Limbara - granite mountains in northern Sardinia

Monte Limbara ⭐️ · 🗺️Direction·🌐Website·📞

Where: near Tempio Pausania
Best for: northern Sardinia, granite scenery, panoramic walks

Monte Limbara gives you a completely different mountain environment: granite formations, forests and broad views across northern Sardinia.

It's a good option if you're staying around Costa Smeralda / Olbia / Tempio Pausania and don't want to drive all the way to the central-east mountains.

🌿 6. Tepilora Regional Park - forests and rivers

Tepilora is one of Sardinia's regional parks and covers nearly 7,900 hectares. The landscape combines forest, granite and limestone cliffs and waterways, making it particularly good for a more relaxed nature experience.

It's northeast of the main Gennargentu/Supramonte mountain zone and can be combined nicely with the inland areas around Bitti and Posada.

The best mountain areas in Sardinia are the rugged Gennargentu range (with Punta La Marmora, the island’s highest peak) and the Supramonte massif, while the top natural parks include Parco Nazionale del Golfo di Orosei e del Gennargentu, Parco di Porto Conte near Alghero, and Parco Naturale Regionale di Tepilora. These combine dramatic landscapes, hiking opportunities, and unique wildlife.

🌿 Natural Parks and Protected Areas

  • Parco Nazionale del Golfo di Orosei e del Gennargentu
    Covers much of central Sardinia, including Supramonte and Gennargentu. Famous for Gorropu Canyon (Europe’s deepest), rare mouflon sheep, and pristine beaches along the Gulf of Orosei.

  • Parco Naturale Regionale di Tepilora
    Located in Nuoro province, it features forests, rivers, and wetlands. Great for hiking, cycling, and birdwatching.

  • Parco di Porto Conte (near Alghero)
    Coastal cliffs, caves (Neptune’s Grotto), and Monte Doglia. Combines sea and mountain scenery.

  • Parco Regionale di Molentargius – Saline (near Cagliari)
    Famous for pink flamingos and saltwater lagoons, though less mountainous, it’s a unique ecosystem.

⚠️ Practical Considerations

  • Accessibility: Gennargentu and Supramonte require good fitness; trails can be steep and rocky.
  • Seasonality: Summer can be very hot; spring and autumn are ideal for hiking.
  • Wildlife: Mouflon, golden eagles, and endemic plants are highlights, but respect conservation rules.
  • Risks: Some areas (Supramonte, Gorropu Canyon) are remote with limited phone coverage—always carry water and maps.

👉 If you want challenging hikes and wild scenery, choose Supramonte and Gennargentu. For easier trails and mixed coastal views, Porto Conte and Monte Limbara are excellent.

The best mountain areas and parks in Sardinia center on the rugged interior, especially the Gennargentu massif and Supramonte, with strong options in the north, south, and other ranges. Sardinia’s mountains are wilder and less crowded than its famous beaches, offering hiking, canyons, forests, wildlife (mouflon, Sardinian deer, golden eagles), and ancient Nuragic sites.

Top Mountain Areas & Parks

Gennargentu National Park (Parco Nazionale del Golfo di Orosei e del Gennargentu)
This is the standout for mountains. Covering ~74,000 hectares in central-eastern Sardinia (Nuoro/Ogliastra provinces), it includes the island’s highest peaks and extends to the dramatic Gulf of Orosei coastline.

  • Key peaks: Punta La Marmora (1,834 m, Sardinia’s highest), Bruncu Spina (~1,829 m).
  • Highlights: Deep gorges, forests, remote villages, wildlife, and trails. Note that the park was formally established in 1998 but is not fully operational as an administrative entity—protection and access still apply.
  • Best for serious hiking and solitude.

Supramonte
A vast limestone massif (part of the broader Gennargentu area) in central-eastern Sardinia, known for dramatic plateaus, sheer cliffs, caves, canyons, and forests.

  • Standout feature: Gola di Gorropu (one of Europe’s deepest canyons, with walls up to ~500 m).
  • Other draws: Monte Corrasi, Lanaittu Valley, Tiscali Nuragic village (hidden in a sinkhole), and trails ranging from moderate to challenging (including sections of the multi-day Selvaggio Blu coastal trek).
  • Excellent for adventurous hikers; some routes require guides or good fitness.

Monte Limbara / Limbara Mountain Park
Northern Sardinia (Gallura/Logudoro), with the main peak at 1,362 m. Features granite formations, pine and cork oak forests (some sequoias), and family-friendly to moderate trails. Good for panoramic views and less extreme hiking than Gennargentu.

Monti del Sulcis & Monte Arcosu WWF Oasis
Southern Sardinia (near Cagliari). The Sulcis range includes the large WWF-managed Monte Arcosu reserve (~3,600 ha), focused on forests, streams, and wildlife (notably Sardinian deer). Trails vary from easy walks to more demanding ones; popular on weekends.

Parco dei Sette Fratelli (Seven Brothers Park)
Southeastern Sardinia in the Sarrabus mountains. Large protected area with peaks around 1,000 m, dense forests, valleys, wildlife (deer, mouflon, wild boar), hiking trails, and some Nuragic ruins. Accessible from the Cagliari area.

Other notable mountain areas

  • Monte Linas – Marganai – Oridda: Southwestern granite peaks (Monte Linas ~1,236 m), forests, waterfalls, and scenic beauty.
  • Monte Ferru (Montiferru): Volcanic range in the west (~1,050 m highest), with quieter trails and cooler elevations.
  • Monte Arci: Isolated southwestern volcanic massif with basalt towers, forests, and Nuragic sites—good for warmer-weather hiking.
  • Marghine/Goceano: North-central highlands with peaks up to ~1,200 m and many Nuraghe.

Practical Tips

  • Best seasons: Spring (March–May) and autumn for hiking—cooler temperatures, wildflowers/waterfalls, and fewer crowds. Avoid peak summer heat (July–August) on exposed trails; winters can bring snow on higher peaks (limited skiing at Bruncu Spina).
  • Access: A car is essential for most inland areas. Base yourself in towns like Nuoro, Dorgali, Oliena, Fonni, Baunei, or (for the south) near Cagliari.
  • Activities: Hiking is the main draw (trails vary widely in difficulty). Climbing is popular in Supramonte and around Baunei/Cala Gonone. Wildlife watching and combining mountain days with Gulf of Orosei boat trips or beaches work well.
  • Preparation: Many trails are rugged and poorly marked in places—check local conditions, carry water/map/GPS, and consider guided options for canyons or remote routes. Respect protected areas and leave no trace.

Gennargentu + Supramonte form the clear “best” core for mountain experiences, while Limbara, Monte Arcosu, and Sette Fratelli offer excellent, more accessible alternatives depending on your base and fitness level.
