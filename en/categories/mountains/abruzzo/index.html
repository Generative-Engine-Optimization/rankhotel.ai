Abruzzo is unusually mountain-heavy: about two-thirds of the region is above 750 m, with three national parks and the Sirente-Velino Regional Park.

  • Gran Sasso & Campo Imperatore - dramatic high mountains, huge open plateau, and Italy’s highest Apennine peak, Corno Grande (2,912 m). Great for spectacular scenery and serious hiking.
  • Majella National Park - wilder and more rugged, with deep gorges, forests, hermitages and Monte Amaro (2,793 m). The Orfento Valley is particularly beautiful.

The best mountain areas and parks in Abruzzo are the Gran Sasso & Monti della Laga National Park, the Maiella National Park, the Abruzzo, Lazio & Molise National Park, and the Sirente-Velino Regional Park. These four protected areas form the “green heart” of the Apennines, offering dramatic peaks, wildlife, medieval villages, and year-round outdoor activities.

Key Mountain Parks in Abruzzo

Park / Area Highlights Activities Unique Features
Gran Sasso & Monti della Laga National Park Corno Grande (2,912 m, highest Apennine peak), Campo Imperatore plateau (“Little Tibet”) Hiking, skiing, cycling, climbing Calderone Glacier, alpine lakes, historic villages like Rocca Calascio
Maiella National Park Mount Amaro (2,793 m), deep valleys, hermitages Trekking, wildlife spotting Known as the “Sacred Mountain,” rich in caves and ancient sanctuaries
Abruzzo, Lazio & Molise National Park Marsican brown bears, Apennine wolves, thick forests Hiking, wildlife watching Italy’s oldest national park (1923), villages like Pescasseroli and Barrea
Sirente-Velino Regional Park Sirente and Velino massifs, alpine meadows Skiing, horseback riding, hiking Less crowded, excellent for both summer and winter sports

Other Notable Mountain Areas

  • Riserva Naturale La Camosciara – Scenic preserve with accessible trails and abundant wildlife.
  • Campo Felice, Roccaraso, Rivisondoli, Ovindoli – Popular ski resorts with modern facilities.
  • Rocca Calascio – Iconic fortress at 1,460 m, famous for panoramic views and film appearances.
  • Campo Imperatore Plateau – Vast high plain with dramatic landscapes, nicknamed “Little Tibet.”

Risks & Considerations

  • Weather extremes: High-altitude areas like Gran Sasso and Campo Imperatore can be unpredictable, with sudden storms even in summer.
  • Wildlife encounters: While rare, Marsican bears and wolves inhabit these parks; follow local guidelines.
  • Accessibility: Some hermitages and trails (e.g., Monte Amaro) require advanced fitness and proper gear.
  • Seasonality: Ski resorts thrive in winter, while hiking and cycling are best in spring–autumn.

Final Thoughts

Abruzzo is often called the “green lung of Italy” because nearly a third of its territory is protected. If you want dramatic alpine scenery, head to Gran Sasso; for spiritual and cultural landscapes, explore Maiella; for wildlife and history, visit the Abruzzo National Park; and for quiet alpine sports, choose Sirente-Velino. Together, these parks make Abruzzo one of Europe’s most diverse mountain destinations.

Gemini

Known as the "greenest region in Europe," Abruzzo protects over a third of its territory through an incredible network of pristine wilderness. The region is defined by three spectacular national parks , a regional park , and dozens of specialized nature reserves that guard the highest peaks of the Apennines and host rare European wildlife like Marsican brown bears and Apennine wolves.

The Three Majestic National Parks

Gran Sasso and Monti della Laga National Park   The largest national park in central Italy, dominated by the Corno Grande , the highest peak in the Apennines at 2,912 meters. A definitive highlight here is Campo Imperatore, a vast, high-altitude alpine meadow famously dubbed Italy's "Little Tibet". Best for: High-altitude alpine hiking, road cycling, and cinematic landscapes. Wildlife: Abruzzo chamois, wolves, and golden eagles.

Parco Nazionale della Maiella   Known as the "Mother Mountain," the Majella massif features Monte Amaro , the second-highest peak in the Apennines. This park is celebrated for its deep limestone canyons, dense beech forests, and historic stone hermitages carved directly into the rock faces. Best for: Rugged mountain trekking, visiting ancient spiritual sanctuaries, and canyon exploration. Note: Dogs are restricted on the main trail networks to protect the local ecosystem.

National Park of Abruzzo   Italy's oldest and wildest park, managed from the alpine hub town of Pescasseroli. It is a heavily forested wonderland world-renowned as the exclusive home of the critically endangered Marsican Brown Bear . Best for: Wildlife tracking, family-friendly valley walks, and exploring medieval lakeside villages like Barrea. Highlight: The breathtaking views around Lake Scanno, a natural heart-shaped lake nestled in the mountains.

Grok · grok-3

Abruzzo is one of Europe’s greenest regions (often called Italy’s “green lung”), with about a third of its territory protected. It features three national parks, one major regional park, and many reserves, offering dramatic Apennine peaks, high plateaus, beech forests, wildlife (including Marsican brown bears, Apennine wolves, and chamois), hiking, and skiing.

Main National Parks and Mountain Areas

1. Gran Sasso and Monti della Laga National Park (largest, ~150,000 hectares)

  • Highlights: Highest peak in the Apennines — Corno Grande (2,912 m). Includes the Campo Imperatore plateau (“Little Tibet”) at high altitude, the southernmost glacier in Europe (Calderone), rocky limestone peaks, and the wetter, waterfall-rich Monti della Laga (Monte Gorzano ~2,458 m).
  • Why visit: Vast landscapes for hiking, via ferrata, horseback riding, mountain biking; ski areas (e.g., Campo Imperatore, Prati di Tivo); wildlife and biodiversity. Scenic villages/castles like Rocca Calascio and Santo Stefano di Sessanio.
  • Bases: Assergi, Campo Imperatore, Prati di Tivo.

2. Abruzzo, Lazio and Molise National Park (oldest, established 1923; ~50,000 hectares)

  • Highlights: Classic wilderness with dense beech forests, peaks such as Monte Petroso (~2,249 m) and Monte Marsicano, valleys (Val Fondillo, Val di Rose), lakes (Barrea, Vivo), and the Camosciara reserve. Stronghold for Marsican brown bear, chamois, and wolf.
  • Why visit: Excellent hiking network (hundreds of km of trails), wildlife viewing, charming stone villages. Great year-round (hiking in summer, cross-country skiing in winter).
  • Bases: Pescasseroli (park headquarters and top mountain town), Civitella Alfedena, Villetta Barrea, Barrea, Opi.

3. Maiella (Majella) National Park (“Mother Mountain”; ~74,000 hectares; also a UNESCO Global Geopark)

  • Highlights: Second-highest Apennine peak — Monte Amaro (2,793 m). Vast high plateaus, deep gorges/canyons (e.g., Valle dell’Orfento), caves, and ancient rock hermitages. Includes Morrone, Monti Pizi, and Monte Porrara.
  • Why visit: Wild, spiritual atmosphere with hiking of all levels, geology, and cultural sites. Quieter and more rugged in places than the others.
  • Bases: Areas near Pacentro, Fara San Martino, Caramanico Terme, or Pescocostanzo.

4. Sirente-Velino Regional Nature Park

  • Highlights: Monte Velino (2,487 m) and Monte Sirente; more accessible mountain scenery with good hiking and ski resorts.
  • Why visit: Convenient for day trips or combined with other parks; popular for both summer trails and winter sports.
  • Bases: Ovindoli, Campo Felice.

Other Notable Mountain Spots & Resorts

  • Roccaraso / Rivisondoli / Pescocostanzo: Largest ski area in central Italy; also excellent summer hiking base near the Abruzzo and Maiella parks.
  • Campo Imperatore (in Gran Sasso): High plateau for dramatic scenery and activities.
  • Monti della Laga (within Gran Sasso park): Waterfalls, forests, and quieter trails.
  • Lakes such as Campotosto (in Gran Sasso area) for walks, cycling, and views.

Practical Tips

  • Best seasons: Summer and early autumn for hiking (long days, wildflowers); winter for skiing/snowshoeing; spring for waterfalls and fewer crowds.
  • Activities: Extensive marked trails for all levels, wildlife watching, climbing, cycling, and horseback riding. Many routes start from park villages.
  • Wildlife note: Respect protected zones and keep distance from animals (especially bears).
  • Access: Most parks are reachable within 1.5–3 hours from Rome or Pescara. Bases like Pescasseroli, Assergi, or Roccaraso make good hubs.

These four protected areas form the core of Abruzzo’s mountain experience and rank among Italy’s finest for nature and outdoor adventure. Official park websites and local visitor centers provide up-to-date trail maps, permits (where needed), and guided options.
