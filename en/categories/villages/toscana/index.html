We asked

«what are the most beautiful villages in Tuscany?»

ChatGPT · gpt-5-6

Tuscany has an almost absurd number of beautiful borghi (hilltop villages), so “most beautiful” is subjective. But if you're looking for the places that combine architecture, landscape, atmosphere, and that quintessential Tuscan feeling, these are excellent choices. The regional tourism board itself highlights many of them among Tuscany's standout villages.

🌿 My shortlist

  1. Pienza - best for classic Val d'Orcia beauty
    A tiny Renaissance town surrounded by the rolling hills of Val d'Orcia. The historic center was designed as an "ideal city" in the 15th century, and the surrounding landscape is UNESCO-listed.
    Don't miss: the views from Via dell'Amore and Via del Casello.

  2. San Gimignano - best for medieval architecture
    Famous for its medieval towers-13 remain today-and an exceptionally preserved historic center. It's a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
    Don't miss: Piazza della Cisterna and the view from Torre Grossa.

  3. Pitigliano - most dramatic
    This is the one that makes you stop and stare: houses seem to grow directly out of a huge tuff-rock cliff. It's known as “Little Jerusalem” because of its historic Jewish heritage.
    Don't miss: seeing it from the road as you approach-the panorama is spectacular.

  4. Montepulciano - best for wine + views
    A beautiful Renaissance hill town surrounded by vineyards and the Val di Chiana. It's also the home of Vino Nobile di Montepulciano.
    Don't miss: Piazza Grande and one of the historic underground wine cellars.

  5. Montemerano - most romantic/underrated
    A tiny medieval village in southern Tuscany with stone lanes, flower-filled balconies and fortified walls. It has a wonderfully intimate atmosphere.

  6. Anghiari - best for an authentic medieval feel
    Built dramatically along a steep ridge, with narrow lanes, arches, stone buildings and expansive views over the countryside. It's officially included among Italy's Borghi più belli.

  7. Montalcino - best for wine-country scenery
    A hilltop medieval town surrounded by vineyards, particularly famous for Brunello di Montalcino. The views across the Val d'Orcia are superb.

  8. Monteriggioni - best tiny fortress village
    Rather than a sprawling town, Monteriggioni is essentially a beautifully preserved medieval walled settlement, making it particularly atmospheric.

  9. Sovana - best for quiet, ancient Tuscany
    Tiny and atmospheric, with medieval buildings and important Etruscan archaeological remains nearby. It's a good alternative if you want somewhere much quieter than San Gimignano.

  10. Barga - best mountain setting
    In northern Tuscany, Barga sits against the Apuan Alps. Its compact medieval center culminates at the cathedral with views across the mountains.

🗺️ If you only have 2-3 days

I'd concentrate on Val d'Orcia rather than trying to cover the whole region:

Pienza → Monticchiello → Bagno Vignoni → Montalcino → San Quirico d'Orcia

San Quirico d'Orcia and Bagno Vignoni are particularly good additions for the scenery and atmosphere.

And if you have time for one more dramatic detour, go south to Pitigliano + Sovana + Montemerano.

For pure postcard scenery: Pienza / Val d'Orcia
For medieval atmosphere: San Gimignano
For dramatic scenery: Pitigliano
For romance and quiet: Montemerano
For wine: Montepulciano + Montalcino

✨ Why These Villages Stand Out

  • San Gimignano is often called the "Medieval Manhattan" because of its tall towers. Walking through its cobbled streets feels like stepping back in time.
  • Montemerano is less touristy, offering a quiet, romantic atmosphere with stone houses and winding alleys.
  • Pienza was redesigned by Pope Pius II in the 15th century as the "ideal Renaissance city," making it a masterpiece of urban planning.
  • Montefioralle is one of the smallest villages in Tuscany, but its authenticity and charm make it unforgettable.
  • Pitigliano is perched dramatically on volcanic rock, with a fascinating blend of Tuscan and Jewish history.

⚠️ Things to Keep in Mind

  • Tourist Crowds: San Gimignano and Pienza can be very busy in summer. Visiting early morning or late evening helps avoid crowds.
  • Accessibility: Some villages like Pitigliano and Montefioralle have steep streets, so comfortable shoes are essential.
  • Seasonal Beauty: Spring and autumn are the best times to visit, with mild weather and vibrant landscapes.

🌍 Suggested Itinerary

If you want a balanced experience:

  1. San Gimignano for history and towers.
  2. Pienza for Renaissance elegance and food.
  3. Pitigliano for dramatic scenery.
  4. Montefioralle for authentic Tuscan countryside.
  5. End in Montemerano with a relaxing visit to Saturnia hot springs nearby.

This mix gives you medieval, Renaissance, natural, and cultural highlights all in one trip.

Gemini

Tuscany is home to some of the world's most perfectly preserved medieval and Renaissance villages, each offering a distinct blend of architectural beauty, rolling landscape views, and deep-rooted culinary traditions. Whether you are drawn to towering medieval skylines, Renaissance urban planning, or quiet cliffside paths, these villages capture the quintessential romance of the Italian countryside.

Iconic Medieval & Renaissance Gems

San Gimignano   Known as the "Medieval Manhattan," San Gimignano is famous worldwide for its striking skyline featuring 14 preserved medieval stone towers. The Vibe: A bustling, vibrant hilltop town enclosed by 13th-century walls. Insider Tip: Skip the midday crowds by wandering down to the tranquil Fonti Medievali—a 13th-century public arched stone cistern—or grab a world-famous saffron gelato at the central Piazza della Cisterna.

Pienza   Rebuilt by Pope Pius II as the "ideal Renaissance town," Pienza offers a masterclass in harmonious 15th-century urban design. The Vibe: Romantic, artistic, and elegantly proportioned, boasting sweeping views over the iconic clay hills of the Val d'Orcia. Insider Tip: Stroll down evocative pathways like Via dell'Amore (Love Street) and be sure to sample the town’s signature aged Pecorino cheese at artisanal shops lining Piazza Pio II.

Montepulciano   Perched high on a limestone ridge, Montepulciano is a grander hill town defined by elegant Renaissance palaces and deep wine caves. The Vibe: A lively, authentic "lived-in" village famous for its rich red Vino Nobile di Montepulciano. Insider Tip: Climb up to the historic Fortezza Medicea at the edge of town, where a sleek local wine bar allows you to sample regional vintages on an outdoor deck overlooking the sunset.

Volterra   Steeped in ancient history, Volterra sits off the beaten path and layers an impressive Etruscan heritage beneath its dramatic medieval exterior. The Vibe: Moody, atmospheric, and timeless, surrounded by deep clay valleys and jagged cliffs. Insider Tip: Visit the well-preserved 1st-century BCE Roman Theater and look for small workshops where local artisans still carve native alabaster stone using centuries-old techniques.

Charming Fortified Hamlets & Hidden Gems

Monteriggioni   An incredibly intact, circular walled fortress built by the Sienese in the 13th century to guard their northern frontier. The Vibe: Small and hyper-focused, it feels like stepping directly into a military fairytale. Insider Tip: You can explore the entire village in less than an hour. Walk along the top of the defensive walls for a magnificent view of the Chianti vineyards, or visit during July to experience its lively Medieval Festival of Monteriggioni.

Montefioralle   Considered one of the oldest and smallest villages in the Chianti wine region, Montefioralle is still enclosed by its original defensive layout. The Vibe: Intimate, peaceful, and nearly car-free, characterized by winding stone alleys draped in ivy. Insider Tip: It is a perfect, short-stop pairing with nearby Greve in Chianti. Walk up to the highest point at Santo Stefano church for panoramic vineyard photos or coordinate a tasting at the small family-run Montefioralle Winery.

San Quirico d'Orcia   A hidden gem along the ancient Via Francigena pilgrimage route, San Quirico d'Orcia avoids the dense crowds of its neighbors while retaining immense character. The Vibe: Serene, understated, and quintessentially Tuscan. Insider Tip: Take a quiet walk inside the Horti Leonini—a beautifully manicured 16th-century Italian Renaissance garden nestled right behind the village walls.

Pitigliano   Known dramatically as "The Little Jerusalem," Pitigliano is spectacularly carved directly out of massive volcanic tufa cliffs in southern Tuscany. The Vibe: Breathtaking, rugged, and completely unique from the typical rolling-hill towns of the north. Insider Tip: Arrive just before dusk; the view of the sandstone town lit up against the sunset from the opposing highway bend is arguably the most dramatic viewpoint in all of Italy.

Grok · grok-3

Tuscany is packed with stunning hilltop and medieval villages (borghi), many recognized by the official association I Borghi più Belli d’Italia (Italy’s Most Beautiful Villages). This curated list requires small historic centers with strong architectural integrity and heritage value; Tuscany has around 29–30 members.

Here are some of the most frequently celebrated and beautiful ones, drawing from travel guides, official tourism sites, and the Borghi association:

Iconic & Must-See

  • Pienza (Val d’Orcia) — A Renaissance ideal town redesigned in the 15th century by Pope Pius II. Perfectly proportioned streets, views over the UNESCO-listed Val d’Orcia hills, and excellent pecorino cheese. Often ranked among the absolute top.
  • San Gimignano — Famous for its dramatic skyline of 14 surviving medieval towers (once ~72). A classic “Manhattan of the Middle Ages” with Vernaccia wine and saffron specialties. Very popular, so visit early or late.
  • Pitigliano (Maremma) — Dramatically perched on a volcanic tuff cliff; nicknamed “Little Jerusalem” for its historic Jewish quarter. One of the most photogenic and unique silhouettes in Tuscany.
  • Montepulciano — Elegant hilltop town famous for Vino Nobile wine, Renaissance architecture, and sweeping views over Valdichiana and Val d’Orcia.
  • Montalcino — Home of Brunello wine, set on a hill with a fortress and classic Val d’Orcia landscapes.

Other Standouts

  • Bagno Vignoni — Tiny and magical; its central “piazza” is a large Renaissance thermal pool fed by hot springs. Unforgettable and atmospheric.
  • Anghiari — Beautifully preserved medieval village in the Valtiberina, known for the historic Battle of Anghiari (immortalized by Leonardo). Steep stone streets and authentic charm; frequently tops “most beautiful” lists.
  • Monteriggioni — Perfectly preserved circular medieval walls and towers (mentioned by Dante). Compact and atmospheric, especially at sunset or during its medieval festival.
  • San Quirico d’Orcia — Gateway to classic Val d’Orcia scenery (cypress-lined roads, rolling hills). Features Renaissance gardens (Horti Leonini) and the nearby Cappella di Vitaleta.
  • Volterra — Ancient Etruscan roots, alabaster workshops, Roman theater, and a quieter, more atmospheric feel than some of the busier spots.
  • Cortona — Hilltop views over the Val di Chiana and Lake Trasimeno; famous from Under the Tuscan Sun.
  • Barga (Garfagnana) — Northern Tuscan gem with mountain views, a cathedral, and a strong cultural scene (including a jazz festival).

More Hidden Gems from the Official List & Guides

Other highly regarded Borghi include Sovana and Sorano (near Pitigliano, tuff villages with Etruscan sites), Santa Fiora, Poppi (Casentino, with a striking castle), Lucignano (“pearl of the Valdichiana”), Buonconvento, Cetona, San Casciano dei Bagni (thermal springs), Montefioralle (Chianti), Capalbio, and Suvereto.

Tips:
The Val d’Orcia (Pienza, San Quirico, Montalcino, Bagno Vignoni) and the southern Maremma (Pitigliano area) deliver the classic postcard landscapes. Northern areas like Garfagnana (Barga) offer greener, more mountainous scenery. A car is ideal for exploring freely. Spring and autumn are best to avoid peak summer crowds and heat.

Beauty is subjective, but these consistently rank highest across official designations, tourism boards, and traveler recommendations for their architecture, setting, and preserved character.
