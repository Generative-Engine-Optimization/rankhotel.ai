The best hotels in and around Montalcino range from ultra-exclusive wine estate resorts nestled in the Tuscan hills to historic boutique lodgings right inside the medieval town walls. Because the area is famous for its Brunello di Montalcino wine, many of the best properties double as active, world-class wineries.

If you are looking for absolute luxury, Castiglion del Bosco, A Rosewood Hotel and Castello Banfi - Il Borgo are widely considered the gold standards of the region, offering private estate experiences and exceptional vineyard views. If you prefer to walk to local enotecas and dinner spots without needing a car, staying directly within the village at a property like Drogheria & Locanda Franci or Palazzo Saloni is your best choice.

Grok · grok-3

Here are some of the best hotels in and around Montalcino (Tuscany), known for Brunello di Montalcino wine, hilltop views, and Val d’Orcia scenery. Rankings draw from consistent high guest scores on Tripadvisor, Booking.com, Trip.com, and mentions in outlets like Condé Nast Traveler.

Luxury / 5-star standouts

  • Rosewood Castiglion del Bosco — A restored medieval borgo (hamlet) on a large private estate with its own Brunello vineyards, in the Val d’Orcia UNESCO area (about 15–20 minutes from Montalcino town). Features elegant suites and villas, infinity pools with panoramic views, a spa, golf, cooking school, and excellent dining (including a Michelin-starred option). Frequently praised for atmosphere, service, and wine experiences; often listed among Tuscany’s top properties. Ideal for a full-resort luxury escape.

  • Castello di Velona Resort, Thermal Spa & Winery — An 11th-century hilltop castle (roughly 8–12 km from Montalcino) with 360° views over vineyards and Val d’Orcia. Unique for its natural thermal springs—the only castle resort of its kind here—with indoor/outdoor thermal pools, a large spa (OliSpa), on-site Brunello production and olive oil, multiple restaurants, and rooms ranging from historic-style to modern with private terraces/patios (some with thermal bathtubs). Strong reviews for location, spa, and scenery.

  • Castello Banfi – Il Borgo (Relais & Châteaux) — Boutique hotel in a restored 17th/18th-century village next to a medieval castle on the large Banfi wine estate (about 10–16 km south of Montalcino). Around 14 charming rooms/suites with historic character, outdoor pool with vineyard views, wine tastings/tours, cooking classes, a glass museum, and Michelin-recognized dining. Guests love the wine immersion, hospitality, and setting. Open seasonally (typically March–November).

Highly rated mid-to-upscale & charming options

  • Podere Brizio — Well-regarded 4-star agriturismo amid olive groves and vineyards (short drive from town). Spacious rooms, pool, spa facilities, tennis, and excellent farm-to-table dining (homegrown/hunted ingredients). Frequent praise for authentic Tuscan hospitality and food.

  • Drogheria e Locanda Franci — Central 4-star-style locanda near the Fortezza (fortress) in Montalcino itself. Charming rooms, attentive staff, strong breakfast/dinner, and convenient location for walking the town. Consistently high scores (often ~9.5).

  • Villa Le Prata – Residenza del Vescovo — Intimate wine-focused relais with high guest ratings (often 9.8). Peaceful setting with vineyard views and strong service.

  • Mastrojanni Relais — Small luxury property (Small Luxury Hotels of the World affiliation) linked to a noted winery; strong reviews for spa, pool, and setting.

Solid value / well-located picks

  • Si Montalcino Hotel — Modern 3-star with panoramic Val d’Orcia views, outdoor pool, and good facilities a short distance from the center. Frequently called a strong value with friendly service.
  • Hotel Dei Capitani — Central location with excellent views; comfortable and well-reviewed for the price.
  • Hotel Vecchia Oliviera — Near the edge of the historic center, private parking, helpful staff, and solid cleanliness/views.

Tips: Many top properties are estate/resort-style a short drive outside the compact hill town (a car is useful). Central options put you walking distance to the fortress, enotecas, and restaurants. Book ahead in high season (spring–fall), especially for wine-focused or thermal experiences. Prices vary widely—luxury estates often start several hundred euros+ per night, while charming town stays can be more moderate. Check recent reviews and availability on Tripadvisor, Booking.com, or official sites, as ratings and offerings can shift.
