ChatGPT · gpt-5-6

If you mean beautiful for scenery rather than simply large or famous, Tuscany has some wonderful options. My shortlist would be:

  1. Lago dell'Accesa - Maremma

    • Probably the most striking if you want crystal-clear, emerald water.
    • Surrounded by wooded hills and close to an Etruscan archaeological site.
    • Particularly lovely for a quiet swim or picnic. Visit Tuscany specifically describes it as one of Maremma's most picturesque lakes.

  2. Lago di Gramolazzo - Garfagnana

    • My pick for mountain scenery.
    • The lake sits beneath the Apuan Alps, giving it a much more dramatic landscape than the typical Tuscan countryside.
    • Good for a relaxed lakeside day, canoeing, and walking.

  3. Lago di Massaciuccoli - near Lucca/Viareggio

    • Very different: more of a wetland/lake landscape, with reeds, waterways and abundant birdlife.
    • Beautiful at sunset and especially interesting if you enjoy nature and photography.

  4. Lago di Bilancino - Mugello

    • The easiest choice if you're coming from Florence.
    • A large blue lake surrounded by the hills of Mugello, with beaches and opportunities for kayaking, SUP, sailing and swimming.
    • It's man-made, so it doesn't have quite the same wild character as Accesa or the Garfagnana lakes.

  5. Lago di Vagli - Garfagnana

    • A dramatic mountain reservoir surrounded by the Apuan landscape.
    • Particularly atmospheric when viewed from above or from the surrounding hills.

  6. Lago di Chiusi - southern Tuscany

    • A quieter option near Chiusi, with wetlands and birdlife.
    • Convenient if you're exploring Montepulciano, Pienza and the Val d'Orcia.

If I had to choose by scenery

💎 Most beautiful water: Lago dell'Accesa
⛰️ Best mountain setting: Lago di Gramolazzo
🌅 Best for a peaceful sunset: Lago di Massaciuccoli
🏖️ Best for swimming/water activities: Lago di Bilancino
📸 Most dramatic landscape: Lago di Vagli

🌟 Key Considerations

  • Best for recreation: Bilancino — easily accessible, family-friendly, with water sports.
  • Best for nature & birdwatching: Massaciuccoli and Montepulciano.
  • Best for history buffs: Chiusi (Etruscan heritage) and Accesa (archaeological sites).

  • Tourist crowds: Bilancino can get busy in summer, while Chiusi and Accesa remain quieter.

Grok · grok-3

Tuscany has several beautiful lakes, ranging from accessible artificial reservoirs near cities to crystal-clear natural pools, mountain glacial lakes, and peaceful wetlands. While Tuscany lacks the famous large alpine lakes of northern Italy, its lakes offer scenic variety, nature, swimming, and outdoor activities, often with fewer crowds.

Here are some of the most frequently highlighted ones:

Lago di Bilancino (Mugello, near Florence)

One of the most popular and accessible. This large artificial lake (created in the 1990s) has beaches, swimming areas, water sports (kayaking, windsurfing, sailing, SUP), cycling paths, and family-friendly amenities. It sits in green countryside and is a convenient escape from Florence.

Lago dell’Accesa (near Massa Marittima, Maremma)

Often praised for its beauty thanks to remarkably clear, emerald/sky-blue waters and a sandy bottom. It’s a natural lake ideal for swimming, set among hills with nearby Etruscan archaeological remains. It feels more intimate and less developed than bigger reservoirs.

Lakes in Garfagnana (Apuan Alps area)

  • Lago di Gramolazzo: Emerald waters framed by the Apuan Alps (including Monte Pisanino). Good for swimming, canoeing/pedalo, fishing, and mountain scenery; has beaches and facilities.
  • Lago di Vagli: Dramatic artificial lake famous for the submerged village of Fabbriche di Careggine (sometimes visible when the water is lowered). Scenic with surrounding mountains; also known for activities like a long zipline.
  • Lago di Isola Santa: More secluded and picturesque, with a small historic village right on the shore—great for peaceful kayaking or canoeing.

Lago Nero (Abetone / Montagna Pistoiese)

A high-altitude glacial mountain lake (around 1,700 m / 5,500+ ft) reached by hiking. Ideal for nature lovers seeking a quiet, reflective alpine setting with picnic spots amid the Apennines.

Lago di Massaciuccoli (Versilia, near Lucca/Pisa/Torre del Lago)

A shallow wetland lake and nature reserve known for wooden boardwalks, rich birdlife, reeds, and peaceful strolls. Associated with composer Giacomo Puccini (who lived nearby). Excellent for nature observation rather than swimming.

Lago di Montedoglio (Valtiberina, Arezzo province)

One of Tuscany’s largest lakes (artificial). It offers wide-open natural spaces, birdwatching, canoeing (often motor-free in parts), and a sense of open landscape. Good for quieter nature days.

Lago di Chiusi & Lago di Montepulciano (Val di Chiana, Siena area)

Unspoiled natural lakes and oases, excellent for birdwatching, gentle boating (often no motorboats), picnics, and peaceful scenery amid hills. Lago di Chiusi in particular is noted for its tranquil, protected character.

Other notables

  • Lago di Burano (coastal, near Orbetello): A beautiful coastal salt lagoon/nature reserve with dunes, clear waters, and rich flora/fauna—more of a lagoon experience.
  • Smaller options like Lake Santa Luce (nature reserve near Pisa, great for walks and wildlife) also appear in recommendations.

Tips: Many of the best swimming spots are the clearer ones like Accesa, Gramolazzo, and Bilancino. Mountain lakes reward hikers, while wetlands excel for birdwatching and quiet walks. Always check local conditions (water levels, bathing regulations, and access) as some are artificial reservoirs. Spring and autumn often bring beautiful colors and fewer people; summer is peak for swimming.

These stand out across travel guides and local sources for scenery, setting, and experience. Preferences depend on whether you want easy access and amenities, mountain drama, crystal water for a dip, or pure nature immersion.
