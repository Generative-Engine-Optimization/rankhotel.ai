Lago Maggiore is especially good for a mix of beautiful lake towns, grand gardens, boat trips, islands, and mountain views. The easiest base for first-time visitors is usually Stresa, which is well connected and sits right opposite the Borromean Islands.

🌸 1. Explore the Borromean Islands

This is the classic Lago Maggiore experience.

  • Isola Bella - visit the extravagant Borromeo palace and its spectacular terraced Italian garden.
  • Isola Madre - quieter and more botanical, with beautiful gardens and a historic villa.
  • Isola dei Pescatori - the smallest and most atmospheric; wander the narrow lanes and have lunch by the water.

You can easily visit them by boat from Stresa.

⛪ 6. See Santa Caterina del Sasso

On the eastern shore, this extraordinary monastery is built directly into the rocky cliff above the lake. It's one of the most memorable historical sights around Lago Maggiore.

🌊 7. Take a boat day

Rather than simply driving around the lake, spend several hours on the water. Public ferries connect many of the major towns and islands, while private boat tours give you more flexibility.

🌳 8. For something quieter: hike

There are walking routes in the hills around the lake, including trails around Belgirate, the northern shore, and the Alto Verbano. The official tourism organization highlights hiking as one of the area's major outdoor activities.

🇨🇭 9. Cross into Switzerland

If you have a car or are staying several days, go north to Locarno and Ascona in Switzerland. They're a lovely contrast to the Italian side, with promenades, elegant squares and mountain scenery.

If you have an extra day, I'd seriously consider Lake Orta, just west of Lago Maggiore. Visit Orta San Giulio and take a boat to Isola San Giulio; the area is known for its picturesque historic village and island.

Experiences & Activities

  • Island hopping: Visit Isola Bella, Isola Madre (botanical gardens), and Isola dei Pescatori (traditional fishing village).
  • Cultural immersion: Explore medieval castles, baroque villas, and museums showcasing local history.
  • Nature & outdoors: Hiking in Val Grande National Park, boat cruises across the lake, and scenic drives along the shoreline.
  • Adventure sports: Zipline in Aurano, paragliding, or mountain biking around Mottarone.
  • Relaxation: Stroll the promenades in Stresa or Cannobio, enjoy lakeside dining, and soak in panoramic views.

Practical Tips

  • Best base towns: Stresa (classic resort feel), Verbania (gardens and culture), Cannobio (charming old town).
  • Seasonal highlights: Spring for blooming gardens, summer for water sports, autumn for quieter cultural visits.
  • Transport: Ferries connect the islands and towns; driving offers flexibility but parking can be limited in peak season.

Risks & Considerations

  • Crowds: Stresa and Isola Bella can be very busy in summer — consider early morning visits.
  • Weather: Mountain activities like Alpyland or Mottarone hikes depend on clear conditions; check forecasts.
  • Costs: Entry fees for villas and castles can add up; multi‑site passes may save money.

Gemini

Lago Maggiore is a spectacular, expansive destination that straddles both Italy (Piedmont and Lombardy) and Switzerland (Ticino). Offering a fantastic blend of grand Belle Époque elegance, dramatic Alpine backdrops, and lush botanical gardens, it tends to be less congested than Lake Como but equally breathtaking.

The absolute top highlights include touring the theatrical Borromean Islands , wandering the vibrant waterfront of Stresa , and marveling at the sheer cliffside architecture of Santa Caterina del Sasso .

The Borromean Islands (The Absolute Must-Do)

These three famous islands drift just off the coast of Stresa and can easily be reached via public ferries or private water taxis:

Isola Bella: Famous for its jaw-dropping, 17th-century Baroque palace and ten-tiered terraced gardens. White peacocks roam freely among classical statues and rare flora.

Isola Madre: The largest island, offering a peaceful contrast to Isola Bella. It is essentially one giant, lush English-style botanical garden featuring exotic birds and ancient trees.

Isola dei Pescatori: A charming, narrow fishing village with red-tiled roofs and tight cobblestone alleyways. It is completely different in vibe from the other two and serves as the perfect spot to stop for a fresh lake-fish lunch.

Charming Lakeside Towns

Stresa (Piedmont): The undisputed tourism hub of the lake. Walk down Il Lungolago (the grand waterfront promenade) to admire the magnificent historic hotels, stop by a bakery to try the local Margheritine di Stresa cookies, or enjoy a sunset Aperol spritz.

Verbania (Piedmont): Known as the "Garden on the Lake," it hosts the world-renowned Villa Taranto Botanical Gardens, which feature over 20,000 plant species blanketing a massive estate.

Cannobio & Cannero Riviera (Piedmont): Located near the Swiss border, Cannobio boasts a beautiful lakeside lido for swimming and a lively Sunday market. Nearby, you can catch views of the dramatic, ruined Castles of Cannero rising directly out of the water.

Locarno & Ascona (Switzerland): At the northern tip of the lake, you enter Switzerland. Locarno features the grand Piazza Grande and the stunning cliff-hanging Madonna del Sasso Sanctuary. Ascona charms with its tightly packed, colorful houses and upscale artistic vibe.

Grok · grok-3

Lago Maggiore (Lake Maggiore) is one of northern Italy’s most elegant lakes, straddling Piedmont, Lombardy, and a bit of Switzerland. It combines dramatic Alpine backdrops, Belle Époque resorts, historic islands, gardens, and charming lakeside towns.

Must-see highlights

Borromean Islands (Isole Borromee) — the lake’s undisputed star, best reached by ferry from Stresa.

  • Isola Bella: Opulent 17th-century Palazzo Borromeo with lavish interiors, shell-and-pebble grottoes, and dramatic tiered Italian gardens with white peacocks.
  • Isola Madre: Largest island, known for its lush English-style botanical gardens, exotic plants, free-roaming birds/peacocks, and a quieter palace with a puppet theatre collection.
  • Isola dei Pescatori (Fishermen’s Island): The only permanently inhabited one — a picturesque village of narrow lanes, colourful houses, and excellent lakeside restaurants serving fresh fish. Ideal for lunch.
    A combined ticket and hop-on/hop-off boats make it easy to visit all three in a day.

Villa Taranto Botanical Gardens (Verbania)
A 16-hectare horticultural masterpiece with ~20,000 plant varieties from around the world, terraces, fountains, ponds, and giant water lilies. One of Europe’s finest gardens.

Hermitage of Santa Caterina del Sasso (Leggiuno, Lombardy side)
A striking 12th–14th-century monastery complex clinging to a cliff above the water. Accessible by boat + steps, or from above via a long staircase/elevator. Superb views and frescoed chapels.

Rocca di Angera
Medieval fortress with towers, gardens, frescoed halls, and the Museum of Dolls and Toys. Great lake views from the ramparts.

Colossus of San Carlo Borromeo (“Sancarlone”) in Arona
A massive ~35 m bronze statue of Saint Charles Borromeo (one of the tallest in the world for a long time). You can climb inside for views.

Best towns & villages

  • Stresa (“Queen of the Lake”): Elegant lakeside promenade, grand Belle Époque/Art Nouveau hotels, villas, and the main ferry hub for the islands. Perfect base. Evening strolls are magical.
  • Verbania (includes Pallanza & Intra): Larger, more local feel; home to Villa Taranto and good dining.
  • Cannobio: One of the prettiest towns — pastel houses, cobbled lanes, lively promenade near the Swiss border. Great for aperitivo.
  • Arona: Lively southern town with the Colossus and a pleasant waterfront.
  • Baveno: Quieter neighbour of Stresa with nice views and granite architecture.
  • Swiss side: Ascona (charming lakeside square and artist vibe) and Locarno.

Other great things to do

  • Take a lake cruise or ferry — the best way to experience the scenery. The Lago Maggiore Express combines a boat trip with the scenic Centovalli Railway through Alpine valleys.
  • Monte Mottarone (above Stresa): Cable car or drive up for panoramic views of the lake and Alps (sometimes as far as Mont Blanc on clear days).
  • Gardens & villas: Beyond Villa Taranto and the islands, look for Villa Pallavicino (park with animals near Stresa).
  • Outdoor activities: Hiking, cycling along the shores, kayaking/paddleboarding, or exploring Val Grande National Park nearby.
  • Markets & local life: Luino’s colourful market is famous; many towns have lively waterfronts for gelato and aperitivo.

Practical tips

  • Best bases: Stresa (most convenient for islands and transport) or Verbania. Cannobio for a quieter, more authentic feel.
  • Getting around: Ferries are excellent and frequent (especially in season). Trains connect major towns; a car helps for the eastern (Lombardy) shore and inland spots.
  • Time needed: 1 full day covers the islands + Stresa; 2–3 days is ideal to add gardens, the hermitage, and a couple of towns.
  • Season: Spring–early autumn is best for gardens and boats (peak summer can be busy). Mild climate year-round compared with higher Alps.
  • Food: Try lake fish (perch, lavaret), risotto, and local Piedmontese/Lombard specialties. Isola dei Pescatori is perfect for a meal with a view.

Lago Maggiore feels more refined and less crowded than Como in many spots, with a wonderful mix of nature, history, and elegant lakeside living. Enjoy!
