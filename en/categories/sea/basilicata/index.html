«what are the best seaside destinations in Basilicata?»

Basilicata is unusually good for a seaside trip because its two coasts feel completely different: dramatic, rocky Tyrrhenian beaches around Maratea versus long, sandy Ionian beaches from Metaponto to Nova Siri. In 2026, five coastal municipalities have Blue Flag beaches: Maratea, Bernalda/Metaponto, Pisticci, Policoro and Nova Siri.

🌊 1. Maratea - the most spectacular

If you want beautiful scenery rather than just a conventional beach resort, I'd look here first. Maratea has a rugged Tyrrhenian coastline with small coves, cliffs and exceptionally clear water. The 2026 Blue Flag beaches include Santa Teresa, Macarro, Illicini, Nera, Castrocucco and Acquafredda.

A particularly distinctive option is Spiaggia Nera o Cala Jannita, known for its dark volcanic-looking sand/pebbles and rocky surroundings.

Best if: you want a romantic, scenic Italian seaside town and don't mind less expansive beaches.

🏖️ 2. Marina di Pisticci - for long, uncrowded beaches

The Ionian coast changes character completely here: broad sandy beaches, dunes and Mediterranean vegetation. Basilicata's tourism board describes Marina di Pisticci as among the more wild and unspoiled stretches of the Ionian coast.

It's particularly appealing if you dislike heavily built-up beach resorts.

Best if: your ideal holiday is sand, sea, space and very little urbanity.

🌿 3. Policoro - the best combination of beach + nature

Policoro has a long sandy beach backed by Mediterranean vegetation, with plenty of beach facilities. Nearby Bosco Pantano di Policoro adds a genuinely interesting nature component, while the ancient Greek site of Heraclea gives you some history away from the beach.

The 2026 Blue Flag covers both the north and south sections of Lido di Policoro.

Best if: you want somewhere you can spend several days without getting bored.

🏺 4. Metaponto - best for families + archaeology

Metaponto has fine golden sand and shallow, gently sloping water, making it particularly convenient for families. The beach also has plenty of services, restaurants and beach establishments.

The big advantage is that you can combine the beach with the archaeological remains of ancient Greek Metapontum. It's also relatively convenient for visiting Matera.

Best if: you want a traditional beach holiday but also want cultural sightseeing.

🌅 5. Nova Siri - quieter southern Ionian coast

Nova Siri sits at the southern end of Basilicata's Ionian coastline, close to Calabria. Its beaches are sandy and the setting becomes increasingly rural and natural as you head toward the Pollino area.

It also received a 2026 Blue Flag for its Lido.

Best if: you want a relaxed resort-style holiday away from the better-known Italian beach destinations.

⭐ If I were planning a trip

I'd divide it like this:

  • Most beautiful/scenic: Maratea
  • Best beach experience: Marina di Pisticci
  • Best all-round base: Policoro
  • Best with children: Metaponto
  • Best for quiet: Nova Siri
  • Best for beach + culture: Metaponto or Policoro

One useful thing to know: the Ionian coast is much more about long, sandy beaches, whereas Maratea is about small coves, rocks and dramatic coastal scenery. The Ionian stretch from Metaponto to Nova Siri is about 35 km and is generally flat, sandy and family-friendly.

🏖️ Why Basilicata’s Coast Is Special

  • Dual coastlines: Basilicata is unique in Italy for having both Tyrrhenian and Ionian seas.
  • Maratea is often called the “Pearl of the Tyrrhenian”, famous for its dramatic cliffs, Christ the Redeemer statue, and picturesque old town.
  • Ionian resorts (Policoro, Metaponto, Nova Siri) are more accessible, with sandy beaches and shallow waters perfect for families.
  • Cultural richness: Ancient Greek ruins in Metaponto and Policoro add historical depth to seaside holidays.

⚠️ Things to Keep in Mind

  • Accessibility: Maratea’s coastline is stunning but rugged — many beaches are small coves reached by stairs or boats.
  • Crowds: Ionian beaches (Policoro, Metaponto) can get crowded in July–August; early June or September is quieter.
  • Budget: Maratea tends to be pricier due to boutique hotels and exclusivity, while Ionian resorts are more affordable.
  • Transport: The Ionian coast is easier to reach by car or train; Maratea requires more planning but rewards with breathtaking scenery.

✨ Suggested Itinerary

  • Day 1–2: Explore Maratea’s old town, Christ statue, and hidden coves.
  • Day 3–4: Relax in Policoro or Metaponto, combining beach time with archaeological visits.
  • Day 5: Nova Siri for a family-friendly resort experience.

Basilicata is uniquely bordered by two entirely different seas , offering two contrasting coastal experiences. The small western Tyrrhenian Coast around Maratea is famous for dramatic cliffs, hidden rocky coves, and deep azure waters. Meanwhile, the eastern Ionian Coast offers miles of vast, flat, golden sand beaches bordered by lush pine forests, making it perfect for families.

The Dramatic Tyrrhenian Coast: Around Maratea

For a more exclusive, wild, and rugged landscape reminiscent of the Amalfi Coast but without the massive crowds, focus your trip on the Tyrrhenian side.

The Golden Ionian Coast: For Space & History

If you are traveling with children, prefer wide sandy shores, or want to mix beach days with exploring ancient ruins, the Ionian coast is your best choice.

Policoro   The heart of Ionian tourism in Basilicata, loved for its clean, shallow waters and upscale marina. It is directly bordered by the protected Oasis of Bosco Pantano , a nature reserve where sea turtles frequently nest.

Metaponto Lido   A lively resort town with endless sandy stretches and fully-equipped beach clubs. It perfectly pairs with history buffs, as the modern shoreline sits just minutes away from the Archaeological Park of Metaponto and its ancient Greek temple ruins.

Marina di Pisticci   Offering a quieter vibe, this area features 10 kilometers of wide, pristine golden dunes backed by fragrant pine and eucalyptus woods that offer great shade on hot summer days.

Grok · grok-3

Basilicata (Lucania) has two distinct coastlines: the dramatic, rugged Tyrrhenian coast around Maratea (the region’s only stretch facing the west) and the long, sandy Ionian coast in the province of Matera (from Metaponto south toward the Calabria border). Both have Blue Flag beaches for water quality and services, and the region remains relatively less crowded and more affordable than better-known southern Italian coasts.

Tyrrhenian Coast: Maratea (the standout gem)

Maratea is widely considered the top seaside destination in Basilicata. Nestled between mountains and sea with a picturesque historic center, the iconic Cristo Redentore statue overlooking the coast, a harbor, and Mediterranean scrub, it offers crystal-clear deep waters, sea caves, and romantic coves rather than long sandy expanses. Ideal for couples, snorkeling, boat trips, and scenic beauty.

Notable spots include:

  • Cala Jannita (Spiaggia Nera / Black Beach) — Dark volcanic sand/pebbles framed by cliffs and greenery; atmospheric and photogenic.
  • Fiumicello — One of the larger, more accessible beaches with sand/pebbles, services, and family-friendly appeal.
  • Macarro (Cala di Don Nicola / Illicini) — Scenic inlet with clear water, good for swimming and nature.
  • Other coves: Acquafredda area (Anginarra, Luppa, Cala di Mezzanotte), Castrocucco, Santa Teresa, and boat-only spots like Spiaggia d’i Vranne.

Access can involve paths or stairs; some areas are wilder. Book ahead in peak season.

Ionian Coast: Long sandy beaches (family-friendly and spacious)

This flatter, golden-sand coastline (roughly Metaponto to Nova Siri) features shallow waters, pine forests, dunes, and easy access—perfect for families, relaxation, and combining with Magna Graecia archaeology. Several spots hold Blue Flags.

Top destinations:

  • Metaponto Lido (Bernalda) — Long stretch of fine golden sand with beach clubs and free sections, backed by pines. Excellent for families (shallow water). Nearby Greek ruins (Tavole Palatine / Temple of Hera, theater) and archaeological museum make it a cultural beach base. About 45–50 minutes from Matera.
  • Policoro — Spacious sandy beaches (including Lido Nord/Sud) plus the wilder Spiaggia del Bosco Pantano (WWF nature reserve with Mediterranean forest, wetlands, and occasional sea turtle nesting). Combines sea, nature, and the nearby Heraclea archaeological park.
  • Nova Siri Marina — Golden sands, clear shallow water, equipped facilities, and a pleasant promenade. Good for families and quieter stays near the Calabria border.
  • Marina di Pisticci — Long sandy beaches with pine woods, beach clubs, free sections, and the Torre di Mare coastal tower. Often includes spots like Lido 48 or Riva dei Ginepri; near Porto degli Argonauti marina.

Other mentions along this coast include areas around Scanzano Jonico (e.g., Terzo Cavone).

Quick tips

  • Choose based on preference: Dramatic scenery, coves, and deeper water → Maratea. Easy sandy beaches, families, and archaeology → Ionian (Metaponto/Policoro area).
  • Best time: Summer (June–September) for swimming; shoulder seasons for fewer crowds.
  • Pairings: Easy day trips or combinations with Matera (Sassi UNESCO site) from the Ionian side; Maratea pairs well with the surrounding hills and Pollino National Park.
  • Facilities vary—many have lidos (umbrella/sunbed rentals, restaurants) plus free stretches. Parking can be limited in peak periods at popular coves.

Maratea consistently ranks as the most distinctive and beautiful overall, while the Ionian stretch offers reliable, spacious, and practical beach holidays with added historical depth. Both deliver high-quality sea experiences with a more authentic, less touristy feel than neighboring regions.
