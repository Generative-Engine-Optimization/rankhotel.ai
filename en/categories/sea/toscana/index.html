A few standouts

Castiglione della Pescaia is probably the easiest choice if you want a bit of everything. It has a historic hilltop town, long sandy beaches, and shallow, clear water; the municipality highlights both its Blue Flag status and its suitability for families.

Monte Argentario is more dramatic and feels less like a conventional resort. Beaches such as Feniglia, Cala Grande and Bagni Domiziano combine sandy stretches with coves and rocky scenery.

Costa degli Etruschi-around San Vincenzo, Castagneto Carducci and Bibbona-is excellent if you want beaches backed by pine forests rather than a dense resort town. Several beaches in the area are included in Italy's 2026 Blue Flag list.

Forte dei Marmi is the polished, upscale option: elegant beach clubs, hotels, restaurants and the famous pier. Viareggio is livelier and more urban, with a long sandy waterfront and nightlife.

One particularly beautiful wild option is Cala Violina, near Scarlino. It's in a nature reserve and access is limited during high season, with advance booking required.

🏖️ Why These Beaches Stand Out

  • Cala Violina is famous for its sand that "sings" when walked on, thanks to its unique quartz composition.
  • Spiaggia di Sansone offers some of the clearest waters in Tuscany, perfect for snorkeling.
  • Quercetano Bay combines accessibility with charm, making it ideal for families.
  • Cala Civette and Baratti Beach provide a quieter, more natural experience, away from crowds.
  • Playa del Can is one of the few dog-friendly beaches in Tuscany.

⚠️ Things to Keep in Mind

  • Many of these beaches, especially Cala Violina and Cala Civette, require a walk through forest trails or rivers, so bring proper footwear.
  • Parking can be limited in peak summer months — arriving early is recommended.
  • Some beaches (like Sansone) are rocky, so water shoes are useful.
  • Facilities vary: Quercetano Bay and Marina di Castagneto are well-equipped, while Cala Civette is more wild and isolated.

Tuscany’s coastline is one of Italy's best-kept secrets , offering an incredible variety of seaside experiences that rivals its famous inland rolling hills. Because the vibe changes dramatically depending on how far south you drive, the perfect spot depends entirely on whether you want high-end luxury, a bustling classic resort town, wild nature, or dramatic island coves.

If I had to pick the ultimate crowd-pleaser, it would be Castiglione della Pescaia . It perfectly blends a postcard-perfect medieval hilltop castle with clean, award-winning sandy beaches and fantastic seafood.

Here is how the region breaks down, along with the top destinations for every style of traveler.

The Northern Coast (Versilia): High Energy & Luxury

If you want wide sandy beaches, endless beach clubs (stabilimenti balneari), high-end shopping, and vibrant nightlife, this is your spot.

Forte dei Marmi   The pinnacle of Tuscan luxury and sophistication . Backed by the striking Apuan Alps, it features 5 kilometers of pristine golden sand lined with elite, historic beach clubs where you can rent private tents. The town itself is full of Michelin-starred restaurants, designer boutiques, and an elegant, celebrity-frequented nightlife scene.

Viareggio   The quintessential classic Italian resort town . Known for its grand Liberty-style (Art Nouveau) architecture and its famous winter Carnival, Viareggio offers endless stretches of sand and a massive pedestrian promenade bustling with cafes, shops, and seafood spots. It is incredibly easy to reach by train, making it the top pick for a quick day trip from Florence or Lucca.

The Central Coast (Etruscan Coast): History & Nature

Moving south, the coastline changes into a mix of wind-shaped cliffs, dark mineral sands, and deep pine forests that run right up to the water's edge.

A stunning, crescent-shaped hidden bay with calm, Caribbean-clear water and unique silver-and-brown sand. The beach is framed by a pristine pine forest providing natural shade. Right behind the sand lies the Archaeological Park of Populonia, meaning you can easily combine a morning swim with a hike through ancient Etruscan ruins.

The Southern Coast (Maremma & Argentario): Wild, Rugged & Scenic

This is Tuscany’s wilder, more dramatic coast. It is characterized by isolated rocky coves, nature reserves, and deep blue waters.

Castiglione della Pescaia   Consistently voted the best overall beach town in Tuscany . A fortified medieval village sits perched on a hill overlooking a crystal-clear sea and a lively marina below. It provides a wonderful balance: it's fully equipped for tourists with excellent seafood restaurants and services, yet retains an authentic, historic Tuscan soul.

Porto Ercole & Porto Santo Stefano   These two upscale harbor towns sit on Monte Argentario , a dramatic rocky peninsula tethered to the mainland by narrow strips of sand. Porto Ercole is quieter and more romantic, while Santo Stefano is livelier. The coastline here is steep and rugged, hiding secluded, pebble-beach coves like Cala del Gesso that require a steep hike or a boat rental to access.

The Islands: Postcard Perfection

Elba Island   A short ferry ride from Piombino, Elba holds some of the most breathtaking waters in Europe . Beaches like Capo Bianco feature dazzling white pebbles set against towering white cliffs, while Spiaggia di Cavoli offers fine golden sand protected inside a steep, windless cove. It’s a paradise for snorkeling and boating.

Insider Tips for Your Tuscan Beach Trip

Understand the "Bagno" Culture: Much of the northern coast is dominated by private beach clubs (bagni). You pay a daily fee for an umbrella, sunloungers, and access to showers and changing rooms. If you prefer raw nature without paying, head south to the Sterpaia Nature Park or Spiaggia della Lecciona , which are protected free beaches.

Transportation: If you plan to visit Versilia (Viareggio), you can easily rely on trains. However, if you want to explore the hidden coves of the Maremma or the dramatic viewpoints of Monte Argentario, renting a car is essential .

Castiglione della Pescaia (Maremma) stands out as one of the top overall picks, frequently praised for its medieval hilltop old town overlooking long stretches of fine golden sand, clear water, and strong environmental credentials (including repeated Cinque Vele / 5 Sails awards from Legambiente and Touring Club, plus Blue Flag status). It balances charm, family-friendly facilities, and quality services without feeling overly commercial.

Other highly regarded destinations, roughly from north to south:

Northern coast (Versilia & Apuan Riviera)

  • Forte dei Marmi — The glamorous, upscale option with luxury beach clubs, designer shopping, and a celebrity/jet-set vibe. Wide sandy beaches backed by pine woods; exclusive and lively.
  • Viareggio — Classic Art Nouveau resort town with a long promenade, wide sandy beaches, beach clubs, and lively atmosphere (famous for its Carnival). Easy to reach by train and good for first-timers or those without a car. Nearby spots like Marina di Pietrasanta and Lido di Camaiore offer similar sandy stretches.

Etruscan Coast (Costa degli Etruschi, around Livorno to Piombino)

  • San Vincenzo — Soft sandy beaches ideal for families, with good facilities and relatively easy access.
  • Castiglioncello — Dramatic rocky coves and bays (notably Quercetano Bay) with clearer, more scenic waters than the purely sandy stretches further north.
  • Baratti / Populonia — Calm bay with historical Etruscan interest and a more relaxed feel.

Central/southern Maremma

  • Follonica — Lively, family-oriented resort on a wide sandy gulf; more budget-friendly and busy in peak season.
  • Cala Violina (near Scarlino/Follonica) — Standout natural beach in a protected reserve; fine pale sand that “sings” underfoot, turquoise water, and a wilder setting (access often limited to preserve it).
  • Marina di Alberese — Wild, undeveloped beach inside the Maremma Regional Park; dunes, nature, and a free/open feel (great for nature lovers; access often by bike or limited parking).
  • Capalbio area — Dunes, natural beaches, and a more exclusive/hidden-gem vibe; also holds Cinque Vele recognition.

Monte Argentario (southern tip)

  • Porto Ercole and Porto Santo Stefano — Scenic harbors with rocky coves, clear water, pine forests, and an upscale yet atmospheric feel. Nearby beaches include Feniglia (long sandy tombolo) and dramatic spots like Cala del Gesso.

Tuscan Archipelago (islands)

  • Elba — The largest and most developed island option, with varied beaches (e.g., white-pebble Sansone/Capo Bianco near Portoferraio; golden sands at Fetovaia and Cavoli). Mix of lively towns and quieter coves; easy ferry access from Piombino.
  • Isola del Giglio — Smaller, charming island with crystalline water and beaches like Cannelle and Arenella; also holds high environmental awards.

Tuscany’s coast is diverse: the north (Versilia) features long, organized sandy beaches with clubs and nightlife; the center (Etruscan Coast) mixes sand and rock with historical sites; the south (Maremma and Argentario) is wilder, greener, and often clearer-watered. Many spots hold Blue Flag or Cinque Vele awards for water quality and sustainability. Summer is peak season and can be crowded—shoulder months (May–June or September) are often more pleasant. A car helps for the more southern or wilder beaches, while northern resorts are better served by trains.
