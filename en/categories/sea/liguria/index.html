Liguria is a great choice if you want beautiful coastal villages plus swimming, but the experience differs a lot between the western Riviera di Ponente and eastern Riviera di Levante. The west generally has longer, easier beaches; the east is more dramatic, with cliffs, coves and colorful villages.

1. Sestri Levante - probably the best balance

The Baia del Silenzio is one of Liguria's signature seaside settings: a sandy crescent surrounded by colorful houses and a particularly attractive sunset. Sestri also has a second bay, making it more versatile than many of the smaller coastal villages.

Choose it if: you want pretty Italian Riviera scenery and a genuinely enjoyable beach holiday.

2. Monterosso al Mare - best if the beach matters

Of the Cinque Terre villages, Monterosso is the obvious choice for beach time. Fegina Beach is a relatively broad sandy beach and is considerably easier for swimming and lounging than the tiny rocky coves found elsewhere in the Cinque Terre.

Choose it if: you want to combine swimming with exploring the Cinque Terre.

3. Alassio - classic sandy Riviera

If your idea of Liguria is sand, umbrellas, promenade, gelato and a proper seaside-resort atmosphere, look west rather than toward the Cinque Terre. Alassio is one of the classic beach resorts of the Ponente, where beaches tend to be broader and easier to use for a conventional beach holiday.

Choose it if: beach relaxation is more important than dramatic cliffs.

4. Noli - beautiful and less resorty

Noli combines a historic seaside village with a pleasant beach and access to some spectacular coastal scenery. It's a particularly good option if you want something that feels smaller and more authentic than Alassio.

Choose it if: you want village + beach + tranquility.

5. Portofino - best for scenery, not beaches

Portofino is spectacular, but I'd treat it primarily as a scenic destination/day trip, rather than choose it for a beach holiday. The coast around the Portofino promontory is dominated by rocky coves and dramatic scenery; nearby Paraggi has a sandy beach with exceptionally clear water.

Choose it if: postcard scenery, boats, restaurants and walking matter more than having a large beach.

6. Levanto - underrated choice

Levanto sits immediately north of the Cinque Terre and has a much more substantial beach, while retaining easy access to the famous villages. It's a particularly good base if you want to swim in the morning and hike/explore in the afternoon.

🏖️ Key Considerations

  • Luxury vs. local charm: Portofino’s coves (Paraggi, Cannone, Olivetta) are exclusive and scenic, while Noli and Savona offer authentic Ligurian culture with less tourist pressure.
  • Family-friendly options: Levanto and Finale Ligure provide sandy stretches, facilities, and easy access for children.
  • Activities: Levanto is popular for surfing, while Portofino’s coves are ideal for snorkeling and scenic swims.
  • Accessibility: Smaller coves like Olivetta and Cannone may require walking paths or stairs, while Levanto and Finale Ligure are easily accessible by train and car.

⚠️ Risks & Trade-offs

  • Crowds in summer: Portofino and Levanto can be very busy in July–August. Booking accommodations early is essential.
  • Costs: Portofino beaches often charge for loungers and access; free beaches in Finale Ligure and Savona are more budget-friendly.
  • Space limitations: Small coves (Cannone, Olivetta) fill up quickly; arrive early for a spot.
  • Seasonality: The Ligurian coast is best enjoyed May–September. Outside these months, facilities may be closed.

Gemini

The best seaside destinations in Liguria depend entirely on whether you are seeking high-end glamour, postcard-perfect cliffside villages, or wide sandy beaches with a more relaxed, local vibe. The region is naturally divided by Genoa into two distinct coastlines: the rugged, dramatic Riviera di Levante to the east, and the family-friendly, expansive Riviera di Ponente to the west.

The Glamorous & Picturesque East (Riviera di Levante)

Monterosso al Mare (Cinque Terre): While all five Cinque Terre villages offer jaw-dropping vertical views, Monterosso is the only one with a proper, expansive beach ( Spiaggia di Fegina ). It features a lively promenade lined with restaurants, making it the premier choice for travelers who want to combine iconic cliffside exploration with traditional sunbathing.

Sestri Levante: Celebrated for its unique geography, this town sits on a peninsula flanked by two beautiful bays. The iconic Baia del Silenzio (Bay of Silence) is a quiet, crescent-shaped sandy haven framed by colorful pastel buildings, while the larger Baia delle Favole (Bay of Fables) offers organized beach clubs. According to community discussions on Reddit, travelers often note that it feels less overpriced and is far easier to navigate with children than the steep steps of the nearby Cinque Terre.

Camogli: A romantic, authentic fishing village known for its towering, multi-colored seafront houses, historic port, and sweeping pebble beach. It offers a much more intimate, relaxed alternative to nearby hotspots. From here, visitors can take a short ferry to the secluded cove of San Fruttuoso , home to a famous medieval abbey sitting right on the beach.

Portofino & Paraggi: Portofino itself is the crown jewel of Riviera luxury, famous for its upscale yachts, high-end boutiques, and dramatic coves like Baia Cannone . For an actual day in the water, head just five minutes down the road to Baia di Paraggi, which boasts the only true sandy beach in the immediate area and stunning turquoise waters.

The Sandy & Laid-Back West (Riviera di Ponente)

Finale Ligure: A fantastic "sea-to-mountain" destination that pairs wide, golden sandy beaches with a vibrant historic center ( Finalborgo ). It is highly regarded as a hub for both classic beach vacations and outdoor sports like mountain biking and rock climbing.

Noli: A beautifully preserved medieval maritime republic nestled in a sheltered bay. Its main beach, Spiaggia dei Pescatori (Fishermen’s Beach), is wide, sandy, and highly atmospheric, still utilized by local fishermen who pull their colorful wooden boats right up onto the shore.

Varigotti: Just down the coast from Noli, this village stands out for its unique Moorish-style flat-roofed houses painted in vibrant ochre and pink. It is home to Baia dei Saraceni (Saracen Bay), one of the longest and clearest natural sand-and-pebble stretches in the entire region.

Grok · grok-3

Liguria (the Italian Riviera) offers some of Italy’s most scenic coastline, divided into the more rugged, dramatic Riviera di Levante (east of Genoa, toward Tuscany) and the sandier, more resort-oriented Riviera di Ponente (west toward France). It has more Blue Flag beaches than any other Italian region, with crystal-clear water, colorful villages, and a mix of glamorous spots, family-friendly resorts, and quieter coves.

Here are some of the top seaside destinations, grouped roughly by area:

Riviera di Levante (East – more dramatic cliffs, villages, and iconic views)

  • Cinque Terre (Monterosso, Vernazza, Corniglia, Manarola, Riomaggiore) — UNESCO-listed cluster of colorful cliffside villages. Monterosso has the best (and only proper sandy) beach in the group (Fegina), with beach clubs and public areas; the others offer more rocky swimming spots and incredible scenery. Ideal for hiking, photography, and boat trips, though very popular.
  • Portofino — The glamorous icon of the Riviera: pastel harbor, luxury yachts, boutiques, and pine-covered hills. Small beaches and nearby coves (like Paraggi or Niasca). Best as a day trip or short stay; arrive by boat for the full effect. Base yourself in nearby Santa Margherita Ligure or Rapallo for better value.
  • Santa Margherita Ligure — Elegant, romantic town with Blue Flag beaches, seafront villas, and easy access to Portofino (walk, bus, or boat). Good mix of sophistication and practicality.
  • Camogli — Charming traditional fishing village with colorful waterfront houses, a lively pebble beach, and strong local atmosphere. Quieter alternative to Portofino; excellent for seafood and sunset. Nearby San Fruttuoso abbey (reachable by boat or hike) has a unique beach cove.
  • Sestri Levante — “City of the Two Seas,” famous for the postcard-perfect Baia del Silenzio (Bay of Silence) with golden sand framed by colorful houses, plus the broader Baia delle Favole. Excellent for families and classic seaside holidays.
  • Porto Venere (and nearby Lerici in the Gulf of Poets) — Medieval village with dramatic cliffs, pastel houses, and views over the gulf. Often called the “sixth Cinque Terre.” Nearby spots like Cala Maramozza or Fiascherino offer more secluded swimming.

Riviera di Ponente (West – longer sandy beaches, more resort facilities)

  • Varigotti (near Finale Ligure) — Home to the highly scenic Baia dei Saraceni, a crescent of pale sand/pebbles with Caribbean-clear water against cliffs. One of the most photographed and beautiful beaches in Liguria. Nearby Finale Ligure and Noli add medieval charm and more beach options.
  • Alassio and Laigueglia — Long stretches of fine golden sand (among the best sandy beaches in the region), family-friendly, with promenades and a lively yet pleasant atmosphere. Laigueglia is one of Italy’s most beautiful villages.
  • Sanremo — Classic elegant resort with palm-lined promenades, Belle Époque architecture, a historic casino, and beaches. Good base for the western Riviera and close to the French border.
  • Other strong options: Bordighera, Ventimiglia (e.g., Calandre or Balzi Rossi beaches near the French border), and Finale Ligure for a mix of beaches and outdoor activities.

Quick tips

  • Levante suits those who prefer dramatic scenery, hiking, and village charm (beaches are often pebbly or small coves).
  • Ponente is generally better for sandy beaches, families, and more relaxed resort-style holidays.
  • Summer is peak season (crowded, book ahead); shoulder seasons (May–June or September) offer better weather-to-crowd ratios.
  • Many spots are well-connected by train along the coast. Boat trips are excellent for reaching hard-to-access coves like San Fruttuoso.
  • Liguria also has excellent food (pesto, focaccia, fresh seafood) and mild climate.

Top overall picks for most visitors: Cinque Terre (for the iconic experience), Portofino/Santa Margherita/Camogli area (glamour + charm), Sestri Levante (beautiful beaches + atmosphere), and Varigotti (standout natural beauty). Choose based on whether you want hiking/drama, luxury, sandy beaches, or quieter villages.
