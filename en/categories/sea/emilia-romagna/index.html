Emilia-Romagna’s Adriatic coast is great if you like long sandy beaches, shallow water, well-equipped beach clubs, and easy access to restaurants and nightlife. The coast runs for roughly 120 km, with two rather different areas: the lively Riviera Romagnola and the quieter, more natural Lidi di Comacchio.

🌿 My picks by type

For the nicest all-around experience: Cervia
Cervia combines a good sandy beach with a more attractive town atmosphere, historic salt pans and nearby pinewoods. Nearby Pinarella is particularly appealing if you want a quieter, family-oriented beach backed by a large pine forest.

For charm: Cesenatico
This would be high on my list if you don't want a beach resort that feels purely resort-like. Its historic canal port, maritime character and long sandy beaches give you something to explore after the beach. The Levante beach is the main seaside area.

For nightlife: Riccione
Riccione combines a broad sandy beach with restaurants, beach clubs and a strong evening scene. It's a particularly good choice if you want to alternate between relaxing during the day and going out at night.

For the biggest choice of things to do: Rimini
Rimini has more than 15 km of coastline, with everything from family-oriented establishments to beach clubs, plus a substantial historic centre and lots of restaurants and entertainment.

For something more sophisticated: Milano Marittima
This is the more fashionable end of the Riviera, with fine sandy beaches, pine forest, upscale beach clubs and restaurants.

For families and a calmer atmosphere: Cattolica
Cattolica has a relatively compact, well-maintained beach and a quieter feel than Rimini or Riccione while retaining plenty of services.

For nature: Lido di Spina / Lido delle Nazioni / the Lidi di Comacchio
The northern coast is noticeably different from the central Riviera: there are pinewoods, wetlands and the Po Delta landscape, with broad sandy beaches and shallow water. It's a good choice if you want beach time without the intensity of the big resort towns.

If I were choosing a 5-day trip

I'd consider Cervia + Cesenatico as a particularly nice combination: beach and relaxation in Cervia, then the charming historic waterfront of Cesenatico. If nightlife is important, swap Cesenatico for Riccione; if nature is the priority, head north toward Comacchio and the Lidi.

One thing to know: Emilia-Romagna isn't the place to go for dramatic rocky coves or turquoise Mediterranean water. Its appeal is different-very long, sandy, gently shelving Adriatic beaches, excellent beach facilities, and a strong food/social scene.

Gemini

The best seaside destinations in Emilia-Romagna sit along the Riviera Romagnola , a vibrant 110-kilometer stretch of Adriatic coastline famous for its golden sand beaches, pristine organization, world-class hospitality, and historic maritime charm. Whether you are looking for an action-packed resort city, a chic fashion-forward getaway, or a quiet fishing village with authentic local food, there is a perfect spot tailored to your style.

The Top Seaside Destinations in Emilia-Romagna

Rimini    Best for: A mix of historic Roman sights, non-stop nightlife, and wide city beaches. As Italy’s largest beach resort, Rimini balances kilometers of fully serviced beach clubs (bagni) with a surprisingly rich, cobbled old town that honors its history from the Roman Empire up to filmmaker Federico Fellini. It's a great "do-it-all" destination that connects directly to major train lines.

Cesenatico    Best for: Picturesque historic charm and world-class seafood. Built around a striking canal harbor originally designed by Leonardo da Vinci , Cesenatico feels like a classic Italian fishing village colliding with a premium beach town. Skip the sand a little before sunset to walk the canal side and enjoy fresh catch at phenomenal beachside osterias.

Milano Marittima    Best for: Upscale style, luxury beach clubs, and a green garden-city vibe. Originally designed as an exclusive summer retreat for Milanese elite, Milano Marittima stands out for its high-end boutiques, lush pine forests, elegant Art Nouveau villas, and trendsetting beach lounges like Papeete Beach.

Cervia    Best for: Relaxing family holidays and wellness. Directly south of Milano Marittima, Cervia is historic, peaceful, and known for its ancient "sweet salt" pans where wild flamingos congregate. A massive 30-hectare coastal pine forest separate the historic center from the sea, making it incredibly popular for cycling, strolling, and safe swimming.

Riccione    Best for: High-street shopping, theme parks, and trendy social scenes. Known as the "Green Pearl of the Adriatic," Riccione centers around the pedestrianized Viale Ceccarini, a glamorous avenue of high-end shopping and open-air cafes. It features premium, modern beach clubs and is a fantastic base if you plan to visit major water parks like Aquafan.

Cattolica    Best for: Tranquil, shallow swimming and families with small kids. Right on the southern border of the region, Cattolica is dubbed the "Queen of the Adriatic". Protected by a crescent-shaped bay, its waters are exceptionally calm and shallow, backed by a colorful, intimate waterfront promenade and a top-tier aquarium.

Lidi di Comacchio    Best for: Unspoiled nature and wilder, pine-backed beaches. Located further north near the Po Delta Regional Park , these seven distinct beach areas offer a complete change of pace from the buzzing southern towns. Head to Lido di Spina or Lido di Volano for uncrowded, wide sands bordered by nature reserves—perfect for birdwatching and cycling.

Grok · grok-3

The best seaside destinations in Emilia-Romagna center on the Riviera Romagnola (Romagna Riviera), a roughly 110–120 km stretch of mostly fine sandy Adriatic beaches with shallow waters, excellent services, and many Blue Flag awards for water quality and sustainability (around 10–11 locations in recent years).

This coast mixes lively resorts, family-friendly spots, pine forests, and more natural areas near the Po Delta. Key highlights include:

Top Classic Destinations

  • Rimini — The capital of the Riviera and Italy’s classic summer beach destination. It has one of the longest sandy beaches (around 15 km), packed with stabilimenti (beach clubs), activities, nightlife, and hotels right by the sea. The historic center adds Roman and Renaissance culture (Arch of Augustus, Tiberius Bridge, Fellini connections). Ideal for energy, families, and culture mixed with beach time.
  • Riccione — Often called Rimini’s more stylish “sister” or the “Green Pearl of the Adriatic.” Elegant Viale Ceccarini for shopping and strolling, excellent beaches, water parks (Aquafan, Oltremare), and a lively but polished atmosphere with nightlife. Great for a mix of beach, fashion, and family fun.
  • Cesenatico — A charming port town with a canal designed by Leonardo da Vinci and an open-air maritime museum of traditional Adriatic boats. Wide, well-equipped beaches (Levante and Ponente) that work well for families, plus a lively but less overwhelming vibe than Rimini/Riccione.

Trendy & Elegant Spots

  • Milano Marittima (part of Cervia) — The chic, trendier option with stylish beach clubs (famous ones like Papeete for sunset aperitifs), pine forest setting, boutiques, and a sophisticated crowd. More exclusive feel while still very accessible.
  • Cervia (including Pinarella) — Known as the “city of salt,” with historic salt warehouses/museum, a large pine forest (pineta), thermal baths, and Blue Flag beaches. More relaxed and nature-oriented than the big nightlife hubs; excellent for families and a quieter base.

Family-Friendly & Quieter Options

  • Cattolica — Nicknamed the “Queen of the Adriatic.” Long sandy beaches, strong family orientation, the large Aquarium of Cattolica, and a pleasant, less frantic atmosphere.
  • Bellaria-Igea Marina — Frequently praised as one of the best pure family destinations: shallow waters, good services, pedestrian areas, and a calmer pace. Consistently Blue Flag.
  • Misano Adriatico and Gatteo a Mare / San Mauro Mare — Solid, well-equipped stretches between the bigger resorts; good for families seeking convenience without the biggest crowds.

Nature & Unique Atmosphere

  • Comacchio and its 7 Lidos (Lido degli Estensi, Lido delle Nazioni, etc.) — Nestled in the Po Delta Park. Broader, often wilder beaches with dunes, lagoons, and nature focus. Comacchio itself is a lagoon town with canals (“Little Venice” vibe). Quieter and more natural than the southern Riviera.
  • Ravenna’s seaside resorts (Marina di Ravenna, Marina Romea, Lido di Dante, etc.) — Combine beach time with easy access to Ravenna’s world-famous UNESCO mosaics and historic center. Good mix of nature (pine woods, dunes) and services.

Quick Tips

  • Best for — Families (shallow safe waters, kids’ clubs, animation): Bellaria, Cattolica, Cervia, Gatteo, Comacchio Lidos. Nightlife/energy: Rimini, Riccione, Milano Marittima. Nature/culture: Comacchio + Ravenna area, Cesenatico.
  • Practical notes — Most beaches are organized stabilimenti (umbrella + loungers typically €20–30+/day in peak season); free stretches exist. High season is mid-June to mid-September. Easy access by train from Bologna (about 1–1.5 hours to many spots) or the Metromare line between Rimini and Riccione. Many places hold Blue Flags and emphasize accessibility and services.
  • The whole Riviera is highly developed for tourism, so expect excellent infrastructure, but peak August can be crowded—shoulder season (June or September) is often more pleasant.

These stand out consistently across recent guides for their combination of beach quality, amenities, and local character. Rimini and Riccione remain the biggest names, while Cesenatico, Cervia/Milano Marittima, Cattolica, and the northern Lidos offer strong alternatives depending on your preferred vibe.
