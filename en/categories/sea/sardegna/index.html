🌴 1. La Maddalena - my pick for island-hopping

La Maddalena Archipelago is fantastic if the sea itself is the main attraction. The protected archipelago has granite islands, tiny bays and exceptionally clear water. Cala Spalmatore, Spargi and Caprera are particularly worth exploring. Boat trips are a major part of the experience.

A good base is La Maddalena town; there are also hotels such as Grand Hotel Resort Ma&Ma.

✨ 2. Costa Smeralda - for luxury

Costa Smeralda is the famous northeastern coast around Porto Cervo. Think emerald water, granite rocks, beautiful little bays, yachts and upscale restaurants. Spiaggia del Principe, Capriccioli and Liscia Ruja are among the standout beaches.

It's also the most expensive option, particularly in July and August.

For a resort stay, Cervo Hotel, Costa Smeralda Resort is right in Porto Cervo.

⛰️ 3. Golfo di Orosei / Baunei - for spectacular scenery

Golfo di Orosei is where I'd go if you want the most dramatic landscape rather than simply a conventional resort beach.

The coastline has enormous limestone cliffs and famous coves such as Cala Luna and Cala Goloritzé. Many of the best spots are reached by boat or hiking, so this area is less convenient with very young children or if you want to spend the entire holiday walking straight from your hotel onto the sand.

🏖️ 4. Villasimius - easiest all-round beach holiday

Villasimius is in the southeast and is a particularly convenient base for a relaxed beach holiday. You get lots of sandy beaches, relatively calm water, snorkeling opportunities and plenty of accommodation.

There are several large resorts here, including Falkensteiner Resort Capo Boi and Cormoran Resort Villasimius.

🌾 5. Chia & Teulada - gorgeous southern beaches

Chia is an excellent choice if you want beautiful beaches without the Costa Smeralda atmosphere. Su Giudeu, Sa Colonia and nearby Tuerredda combine turquoise water with large dunes and a more relaxed setting.

There are several resort options, including Chia Laguna and Baia di Chia Resort Sardinia, Curio Collection by Hilton.

🏘️ 6. Alghero & Stintino - best combination of beach + town

Alghero is a great choice if you don't want a purely resort-based holiday. It has a beautiful historic center, restaurants and a distinctive Catalan influence, while excellent beaches are nearby.

Maria Pia is particularly family-friendly, while the famous La Pelosa near Stintino has extraordinarily shallow turquoise water.

☀️ If I were planning a first Sardinia trip

  • Most beautiful sea / island hopping: La Maddalena

🏖️ Key Considerations

  • Accessibility: Some beaches (like Cala Goloritzé and Cala Mariolu) require hiking or boat trips, while La Pelosa and Villasimius are easily accessible by car.
  • Crowds: Costa Smeralda and La Pelosa are very popular in summer; for quieter experiences, head to Cala Goloritzé or Is Arutas.
  • Activities: Villasimius and Chia are excellent for water sports, while Costa Smeralda is more about luxury and nightlife.
  • Nature & Scenery: Cala Goloritzé and Cala Mariolu are ideal for dramatic landscapes and pristine waters.

⚠️ Risks & Tips

  • Overcrowding: La Pelosa Beach limits visitor numbers in peak season; booking in advance is required.
  • Environmental Protection: Cala Goloritzé is a UNESCO site — swimming is allowed, but boats cannot dock directly.
  • Costs: Costa Smeralda is expensive compared to other areas; budget travelers may prefer Chia or Is Arutas.
  • Transport: Sardinia’s beaches are spread out; renting a car is the most practical way to explore multiple destinations.

👉 In short: For luxury and nightlife, choose Costa Smeralda; for family-friendly shallow waters, La Pelosa; for dramatic natural beauty, Cala Goloritzé; for water sports, Villasimius or Chia; and for unique sand, Is Arutas.

Sardinia is home to some of the most striking coastlines in the world, with choices ranging from high-end resort towns to rugged, untouched coves. The best seaside destination depends on your preferred style of travel , whether you seek luxury marinas, family-friendly shallows, or dramatic hiking routes along the cliffs.

The top seaside regions and hubs across the island are organized below by what makes them distinct.

The Costa Smeralda & The Northeast: Glamour & Shallow Bays

This region is globally famous for its emerald waters, sculpted pink granite rocks, and upscale lifestyle. It is the most heavily developed area for luxury tourism on the island.

Porto Cervo   The undisputed capital of the Costa Smeralda. Built around a deer-head-shaped natural harbor, it features luxury yachts, designer boutiques, and celebrity-frequented beach spots. While the town itself has no beach, legendary spots like Spiaggia del Principe and Capriccioli beach (East) are just a short drive south.

San Teodoro   Located just south of Olbia, this vibrant town offers a lively night market and excellent restaurants alongside world-class beaches. It is home to La Cinta Beach , a 3.2km stretch of fine white sand backed by a lagoon where pink flamingos reside. Nearby, the managed-access paradise of Cala Brandinchi —often called "Little Tahiti" due to its incredibly clear, shallow turquoise waters—requires online booking in advance to protect the ecosystem.

The Gulf of Orosei & The East: Dramatic Limestone Cliffs

If you prefer wild, raw landscapes and deep water, the central-east coast delivers the most spectacular vertical scenery in Europe.

Cala Gonone   An authentic, seafaring town nestled at the base of steep limestone cliffs. It serves as the primary gateway for boat excursions or intense hikes into the Gulf of Orosei National Park. From the town's marina, you can catch ferries or rent dinghies to reach world-famous coves like Cala Luna (known for its massive sea caves) and Cala Mariolu , which features tiny white marble pebbles and exceptional snorkeling reefs.

The Northwest & Far North: Historic Architecture & Wind-Swept Capes

This region balances postcard-perfect beaches with historic towns that remain lively throughout the year.

Alghero   Known for its distinct Catalan heritage, sea walls, and robust culinary scene, Alghero is widely considered the prettiest historic town on the island. Right on its outskirts sits Spiaggia di Maria Pia , famous for its sand dunes and pine trees. It also functions as a base to explore the dramatic cliffs of Capo Caccia and Neptune's Grotto.

Santa Teresa Gallura   Perched on the northernmost tip of the island overlooking the Strait of Bonifacio toward Corsica. The town itself boasts the beautiful, sickle-shaped Rena Bianca beach right next to the historic center. A mere 4km away lies the wild peninsula of Capo Testa , a wonderland of giant, wind-sculpted granite boulders hiding tiny, clear-water swimming coves.

The South: Sweeping Sand Dunes & Caribbean Aesthetics

Southern Sardinia offers vast coastal landscapes that feel spacious and tropical compared to the rockier north.

Villasimius   The anchor of the southeastern coast, offering a mix of vibrant nightlife and exceptional marine protected areas. It provides seamless access to the white sands of Porto Giunco and the breathtaking Punta Molentis Beach . Punta Molentis is bounded by a promontory featuring ancient Nuraghe ruins and offers calm natural pools between granite rocks.

Chia   A peaceful, relatively undeveloped coastal stretch on the southern tip known for its massive, desert-like sand dunes meeting the sea. Top locations here include Su Giudeu Beach —a surfer and scuba diver favorite with a shallow seabed—and Spiaggia di Tuerredda, a sheltered, Caribbean-style white sand bay that enforces a strict daily capacity cap to prevent overcrowding.

Sardinia’s best seaside destinations stand out for their turquoise waters, white or pale sand, dramatic cliffs or granite formations, and a mix of accessibility and relative seclusion. Top spots consistently highlighted by travel guides (CN Traveller, Lonely Planet, World’s 50 Best Beaches, and others) cluster in a few key areas.

Northeast – Costa Smeralda & Gallura (glamour + classic beauty)

  • Costa Smeralda (around Porto Cervo, Porto Rotondo): Iconic emerald-green waters and pale golden-pink sand. Standouts include Spiaggia del Principe (Prince’s Beach, a sheltered crescent of fine white sand framed by pink granite) and Liscia Ruja. Expect upscale resorts, beach clubs, and yachts; it can get crowded and pricey in peak season.
  • La Maddalena Archipelago (reachable by ferry from Palau): Protected marine park with granite islands and exceptional beaches such as Cala Coticcio (“Sardinian Tahiti”) on Caprera and the famous pink sands of Budelli (viewable only from a distance for protection). Excellent for sailing and boat trips.
  • San Teodoro / Cala Brandinchi (“Little Tahiti”): Fine white sand, very shallow turquoise water, and pine forests—ideal for families. Nearby beaches like La Cinta are also popular.

Northwest

  • La Pelosa (near Stintino): Frequently ranked among the world’s best beaches—talcum-powder white sand, shallow electric-blue water, and a 16th-century watchtower on a nearby islet. Strictly regulated in high season (advance booking often required) to protect the dunes.
  • Alghero area: Charming Catalan-influenced town with a historic center, plus beaches like Spiaggia di Maria Pia (long white sand backed by dunes and pines, good for families). Nearby Capo Caccia offers dramatic scenery.
  • Santa Teresa di Gallura: Rena Bianca beach right by the town, plus easy access to the northern tip and ferries toward Corsica.

East Coast – Gulf of Orosei (wild & dramatic)

  • Cala Goloritzé (Baunei): Frequently named one of the world’s top beaches (sometimes #1). Powder-white pebbles/sand, crystalline water, and a striking limestone pinnacle. Accessible mainly by boat or a hike; protected status keeps it relatively unspoiled.
  • Cala Mariolu and Cala Luna: Neighboring coves with white pebbles, vivid turquoise water, and cliffs. Best reached by boat from Cala Gonone, Arbatax, or Santa Maria Navarrese. Great for snorkeling and a sense of adventure.
  • Base towns: Cala Gonone or Arbatax for boat tours and lodging.

South & Southwest (long sandy stretches, more relaxed)

  • Villasimius / Porto Giunco: White sand, clear water, and a lagoon that sometimes hosts flamingos. Part of a protected marine area; good mix of beaches and amenities.
  • Chia / Su Giudeu / Tuerredda: Vast dunes, soft white sand, and Caribbean-like waters. Popular yet spacious, with good infrastructure and wildlife viewing (flamingos in the ponds).
  • Is Arutas (Sinis Peninsula, near Cabras): Distinctive quartz “rice-grain” sand in white, green, and pink tones with clear water.

Other notable mentions

  • Costa Paradiso (northwest): Rocky inlets and fine-sand beaches such as Li Cossi.
  • Pula / Santa Margherita di Pula: Convenient for the south, with good beaches and proximity to archaeological sites like Nora.
  • Island escapes such as Carloforte (San Pietro) or Sant’Antioco for a quieter, more authentic coastal feel.

Practical tips

Sardinia has nearly 2,000 km of coastline, so variety is huge—rocky coves in the east and northeast, longer sandy beaches in the south and west. Peak season (July–August) brings crowds and higher prices, especially on the Costa Smeralda and at La Pelosa; shoulder months (June, September) are often ideal. Many of the most spectacular spots (Gulf of Orosei, parts of La Maddalena) are best reached by boat. A car is highly recommended for flexibility. Check local regulations, as several beaches have limited daily access or parking systems to protect the environment.

These destinations combine natural beauty with different vibes—from ultra-luxurious to wild and family-friendly—making Sardinia one of Europe’s premier seaside islands.
