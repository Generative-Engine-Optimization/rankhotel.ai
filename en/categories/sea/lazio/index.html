Copilot · search

The best seaside destinations in Lazio are Sperlonga, Sabaudia, San Felice Circeo, Gaeta, and the Pontine Islands — all known for crystal-clear waters, sandy beaches, and charming coastal towns. For those closer to Rome, Ostia, Fregene, and Santa Marinella are popular, while Tarquinia and Montalto Marina offer quieter escapes to the north.

🌊 Key Seaside Areas in Lazio

1. Riviera degli Etruschi (North of Rome)

  • Tarquinia Lido & Montalto Marina – Wide sandy beaches, family-friendly, with Etruscan archaeological sites nearby.
  • Santa Marinella & Santa Severa – Small seaside towns with medieval castles and calm waters.

2. Riviera di Roma (Near the Capital)

  • Ostia – Rome’s closest beach, lively atmosphere, beach clubs, and nightlife.
  • Fregene & Ladispoli – Trendy spots for Romans, with good seafood restaurants and kite-surfing.

3. Riviera di Ulisse (South of Rome)

  • Sperlonga – Blue Flag beach, picturesque whitewashed village, clear waters.
  • Sabaudia & San Felice Circeo – Famous dunes, natural parks, and excellent for hiking + seaside relaxation.
  • Terracina – Long sandy coastline with views of the Temple of Jupiter Anxur.
  • Gaeta – Seven beaches, historic old town, and excellent seafood.
  • Formia & Minturno – Family-friendly with calmer waters.

4. Pontine Islands

  • Ponza & Palmarola – Dreamlike archipelago with coves, grottoes, and turquoise waters.
  • Ventotene – Quieter, ideal for snorkeling and diving.

⚠️ Practical Considerations

  • Blue Flag 2026: Sperlonga, Sabaudia, San Felice Circeo, and Gaeta are recognized for clean waters and high-quality services.
  • Crowds: Ostia and Fregene can be very busy in summer; islands require ferry bookings in advance.
  • Transport: Northern beaches are accessible by train from Rome; southern Riviera di Ulisse is best reached by car.
  • Costs: Sunbed rentals average €10–20/day in Sperlonga and Gaeta.([8 Best Beaches in Lazio 2026 [Guide]])

🌟 Recommendation

If you want scenic beauty and clear waters, choose Sperlonga or Sabaudia. For a romantic island escape, head to Ponza. If convenience matters, Ostia is the fastest option from Rome, while Gaeta offers the best mix of culture, beaches, and food.

Gemini

Lazio’s coastline hides some of Italy's most remarkable seaside escapes, which are often overlooked by international travelers but deeply loved by locals. The absolute best seaside destinations in Lazio are concentrated along the southern Riviera di Ulisse , led by the whitewashed jewel of Sperlonga , the historic fortress town of Gaeta , and the dramatic dunes of Sabaudia . Further offshore, the volcanic island of Ponza offers some of the most crystal-clear turquoise waters in the entire Mediterranean.

The Jewels of the Southern Coast (Riviera di Ulisse)

Sperlonga   Often called Lazio’s answer to Santorini, this stunning medieval town is perched on a rocky promontory overlooking two long crescents of golden sand. The Vibe: Romantic, elegant, and rich in history. Wander the quiet, whitewashed alleys where the sea suddenly appears between buildings. Don't Miss: The archaeological area of the Villa of Tiberius and its coastal grotto. Trade-off: Parking is notoriously challenging, and the cliffside steps can be steep.

Gaeta   A dynamic coastal city blending military history, medieval architecture, and seven distinct sand beaches. The Vibe: Historically rich and lively, perfect for travelers wanting more than just sunbathing. Best Beaches: Spiaggia di Serapo is the expansive town beach with calm water, while the wilder Lido Trecento Gradini (300 Steps Beach) offers pristine turquoise waters below a cliff. Trade-off: No direct train line; it requires driving or taking a bus from Formia station.

Sabaudia   A favorite summer retreat for fashionable Romans and celebrities seeking privacy and raw natural beauty. The Vibe: Upscale, uncrowded, and wild. The beach features a 15-kilometer stretch of fine sand backed by dramatic coastal dunes and the Circeo National Park. Don't Miss: Strolling along the wooden boardwalks designed to protect the fragile dune ecosystem. Trade-off: Minimal beachfront urbanization means fewer seaside shops or quick-dining options directly on the sand.

San Felice Circeo   Where the rocky headland of Mount Circeo breaks the flat coastline, creating rugged cliffs and deep sea caves. The Vibe: Authentic and outdoorsy, balancing marine relaxation with trekking. Don't Miss: Snorkeling in the small rocky coves around the promontory on a calm day for the clearest water in the region. Trade-off: Beaches are smaller and can get highly congested on summer weekends.

Terracina   A complete resort destination that splits into an ancient upper historic center and a vibrant, modern lower beach town. The Vibe: Active, family-friendly, and culturally immersive. Don't Miss: Catching the sunset from the hilltop ruins of the Temple of Jupiter Anxur for views stretching down to the islands. Trade-off: The modern lower town's architecture leans toward post-war apartment buildings, lacking the cohesive fairytale look of Sperlonga.

The Island Paradise

Ponza   The largest island of the Pontine Archipelago, known for its sheer volcanic cliffs, colorful harbor houses, and glowing grottoes. The Vibe: Laid-back island chic, best experienced by renting a small motorboat to explore hidden coves. Best Beaches: Cala Feola (one of the few sandy beaches) and the dramatic Lucia Rosa Beach. Trade-off: Requires a ferry or hydrofoil connection from the mainland ports of Anzio, Formia, or Terracina.

Best for Quick Day Trips from Rome (Northern Coast)

If you are short on time and relying entirely on public transit from Rome, traveling northwest toward Civitavecchia offers the most seamless train connections.

Santa Severa   Famous for its striking 9th-century Odescalchi Castle which stands directly on the sand where waves lap at its foundations. The Vibe: Peaceful and historical, offering broad stretches of free public beach (spiaggia libera). Trade-off: The water is clean but lacks the deep, crystalline transparency found on the southern Riviera di Ulisse.

Santa Marinella   Lazio's premier surf town, heavily serviced and reachable via a brief 35-to-50-minute train ride from central Rome. The Vibe: High-energy and convenience-focused, with a red-tile boardwalk lined with seafood restaurants. Trade-off: The main sandy beach is small and heavily commercialized; the free public sections are rocky and crowded.

Grok · grok-3

The best seaside destinations in Lazio (the region around Rome) are concentrated along the Tyrrhenian coast, especially the southern Riviera di Ulisse (province of Latina) and the nearby Pontine Islands. The coastline stretches over 360 km and ranges from long sandy beaches and protected dunes to dramatic cliffs, whitewashed villages, and clear waters. Many spots hold Blue Flag status for water quality and services (as of recent 2025–2026 data).

Here are the top standout destinations, ranked roughly by frequent acclaim for scenery, beaches, character, and water quality:

Top Picks (Riviera di Ulisse / South of Rome)

  • Sperlonga — Often called the jewel of the Lazio coast and one of Italy’s most beautiful villages. A whitewashed medieval borgo perched on a rocky promontory with labyrinthine alleys, sea views, and two crescent sandy beaches (including Spiaggia dell’Angolo). Nearby is the Grotto of Tiberius (Roman emperor’s seaside villa). Excellent water quality and Blue Flag status. About 120 km from Rome.
  • Gaeta — The “crown jewel” of the Riviera di Ulisse, with a dramatic historic center on a promontory (Castello Angioino-Aragonese, Santuario della Santissima Trinità / Montagna Spaccata), multiple beaches (notably wide sandy Serapo Beach with the “Nave di Serapo” rock formation), and clear waters. Several Blue Flag beaches. Rich history and scenery.
  • Sabaudia & San Felice Circeo — Within Circeo National Park. Sabaudia features long stretches of protected dunes and wild beaches (Torre Paola for surfing/kitesurfing, Bufalara with grazing buffalo), pine forests, and coastal lakes. San Felice Circeo is a charming hill town on a limestone promontory linked to the myth of Circe and Ulysses, with scenic beaches and dramatic cliffs. Among the cleanest waters and most unspoiled landscapes in Lazio.
  • Terracina — Balanced mix of history and beach life: long, well-equipped sandy beaches, a lively promenade, and the impressive Temple of Jupiter Anxur high above the town with panoramic views. Blue Flag status; family-friendly.

Other Strong Contenders

  • Anzio & Nettuno — Historic towns south of Rome (easy day trips). Anzio has Blue Flag beaches, cliffs, Nero’s Grottoes (swim near ancient Roman ruins), and good seafood. Nettuno offers a medieval center, long beaches, and nearby Torre Astura (picturesque castle by the sea).
  • Santa Marinella (north of Rome) — Known as the “Pearl of the Tyrrhenian.” Soft sandy beaches with calm waters (breakwaters), elegant beach clubs, a marina, and the Castello Odescalchi. Popular and convenient for Romans.
  • Pontine Islands (Ponza, Palmarola, Ventotene) — True Mediterranean highlights with crystal-clear water and dramatic scenery. Ponza features Chiaia di Luna and Cala Feola; Palmarola has stunning Cala Porto; Ventotene is quieter and more authentic. Accessible by ferry (often from Formia or Anzio). Frequently ranked among the region’s most beautiful spots.

Quick Tips

  • Best overall for scenery and atmosphere: Sperlonga, Gaeta, and the Circeo area.
  • For wilder/nature-focused beaches: Sabaudia dunes and Circeo National Park.
  • Closest to Rome / easy day trips: Ostia (urban and lively), Anzio/Nettuno, Santa Marinella, or Fregene.
  • Water quality: Excellent across most of the Latina province and islands (high percentage rated “excellent” by ARPA monitoring); many Blue Flag sites.
  • Summer is peak season and can be crowded—shoulder months (May–June or September) are often better. Many beaches have free stretches plus paid stabilimenti (beach clubs) with facilities.

These destinations combine accessible beaches, history, and Mediterranean charm, making Lazio a strong alternative to more famous Italian coasts.
