ChatGPT · gpt-5-6

Campania is fantastic if you like food, archaeology, dramatic coastline, islands, and lively cities. The region is much more than just the Amalfi Coast.

🌋 The essentials

  • Naples - Spend at least a day wandering the historic center, eating pizza, visiting churches and museums, and walking the waterfront. The historic center is a UNESCO World Heritage site.
  • Pompeii - One of the region's essential experiences: explore the ancient city buried by Vesuvius in AD 79. Archaeological Park of Pompeii
  • Herculaneum - A smaller archaeological site than Pompeii, but exceptionally well preserved. Archaeological Park of Herculaneum
  • Mount Vesuvius - Hike around the crater and look across the Bay of Naples. Vesuvius National Park
  • Amalfi Coast - Explore Positano, Amalfi, Ravello and Vietri sul Mare. The coastline is a UNESCO World Heritage landscape.
  • Capri - Take a boat over for spectacular scenery, the Faraglioni, Anacapri and Monte Solaro.
  • Sorrento - A great base for the coast, Capri and Pompeii, with beautiful views and plenty of lemon-themed food and drink.
  • Caserta - Visit the enormous Royal Palace of Caserta and its gardens, one of Campania's major UNESCO attractions. Royal Palace of Caserta
  • Paestum - Go south for extraordinarily well-preserved Greek temples; it's particularly worthwhile if you enjoy ancient history.
  • Cilento - For a quieter side of Campania, explore the national park, beaches and small coastal villages.

🥾 If you like outdoor activities

The Path of the Gods above the Amalfi Coast is one of the classic hikes, with huge views over the coastline.

You can also explore Punta Campanella, beaches around the Sorrento Peninsula, or the countryside of Cilento.

🍕 And definitely eat

Try Neapolitan pizza, sfogliatella, pasta with local seafood, mozzarella di bufala, limoncello and the region's excellent wines. Campania's food is a major part of the experience.

🗓️ A good 5-day first trip

Day 1: Naples
Day 2: Pompeii + Vesuvius
Day 3: Sorrento + Positano
Day 4: Amalfi + Ravello
Day 5: Capri

If you have 7-10 days, I'd add Caserta, Paestum and Cilento rather than trying to squeeze more into the Amalfi Coast. Campania's attractions range from ancient archaeological sites to beaches, islands and mountain landscapes.

Campania offers a mix of ancient history, breathtaking coastlines, and cultural treasures — highlights include Pompeii and Herculaneum, the Amalfi Coast, Mount Vesuvius, and the Royal Palace of Caserta.

🏛 Archaeological Wonders

  • Pompeii: Explore the ruins of the Roman city buried by Vesuvius in 79 AD. Streets, villas, frescoes, and amphitheaters remain remarkably preserved. 081 857 5347
  • Herculaneum: Smaller but better preserved than Pompeii, with intact wooden structures and mosaics. 081 010 6490
  • Royal Palace of Caserta: A UNESCO site, this 18th-century Baroque palace rivals Versailles with vast gardens, fountains, and a grand staircase. 0823 448084

🌊 Coastal Beauty

  • Amalfi Coast: Famous for dramatic cliffs, pastel-colored towns, and winding roads. Amalfi, Positano, and Ravello are must-visits.
  • Torre dello Ziro: A medieval watchtower offering panoramic views of Amalfi. 338 560 5550
  • Punta Campanella: A protected marine reserve with hiking trails and ruins overlooking Capri. 081 808 9877

🌋 Nature & Adventure

  • Mount Vesuvius National Park: Hike to the crater of one of the world’s most famous volcanoes. 081 865 3911
  • Trails offer views over Naples Bay and the islands of Capri and Ischia.

🎨 Art & Culture

  • Capodimonte Museum (Naples): Houses masterpieces by Caravaggio, Titian, and Raphael in a former royal palace. 081 749 9130
  • Naples itself is vibrant — street food (like pizza fritta), bustling markets, and historic churches enrich the experience.

⚠️ Practical Tips

  • Crowds: Pompeii and Amalfi Coast are busiest in summer; visit early morning or off-season.
  • Transport: Driving is scenic but stressful; ferries and trains are reliable alternatives.
  • Costs: Amalfi accommodations are pricey; inland towns (like Ravello or Caserta) offer better value.
  • Safety: Vesuvius hikes depend on weather; check park alerts before visiting.

Gemini

Campania is a region that hits you with all senses at once , bridging chaotic, vibrant energy with some of the most hauntingly beautiful landscapes and ancient ruins on earth. Whether you are drawn to the dramatic cliffs of the coast, the frozen-in-time streets of Roman empires, or the absolute pinnacle of Italian street food, Campania delivers a bucket-list trip.

To help you plan, I’ve broken down the unmissable experiences by vibe so you can choose the path that fits your style.

🌋 The Ancient & Volcanic Trail

If you want to feel the weight of history, the area around the Bay of Naples offers an unparalleled look into antiquity.

Walk the ghost cities of Pompeii and Herculaneum: Don't just do a quick stop at Pompeii Archaeological Park—it's a massive, living ancient city. If you want a more intimate, shockingly well-preserved look at Roman daily life, pair it with Herculaneum .

Peer into the crater of Mount Vesuvius: Hike up the active volcano that ties the region's entire history together. Walking the trail to the Gran Cono rewards you with massive views of the entire Gulf of Naples.

See the Greek Temples at Paestum: For something that feels entirely different, head south to Paestum. The three massive Greek Doric temples here are incredibly well-preserved and sit in a much quieter, pastoral landscape.

🍕 The Vibrant Core: Naples

You don't just visit Naples; you experience it. It is loud, chaotic, deeply historic, and brilliant.

Get lost down Spaccanapoli: This straight, narrow street cuts right through the historic heart of the city. Wander off into side alleys like Via San Gregorio Armeno, famous for its artisan nativity workshops.

Go underground: Escape the street chaos by descending into Naples Underground (Napoli Sotterranea) to explore ancient aqueducts, bomb shelters, and Roman theater remains hidden beneath the modern city.

Eat relentlessly: This is the birthplace of pizza. Grab an authentic wood-fired pie at legendary spots like Pizzeria Sorbillo, try sfogliatella pastry, and snack on fried street food (cuoppo).

🌊 The Glitz, the Glam, & the Islands

The Campanian coast is legendary for dramatic sea views and pastel-colored towns tumbling down cliff sides.

The Amalfi Coast & Sorrento: Base yourself in Sorrento for easy transit links, then explore the dramatic cliffside towns of Positano, Amalfi, and Ravello .

Capri: The ultimate luxury island getaway. Take the high-speed ferry from Naples or Sorrento, catch a boat to the Blue Grotto, and ride the breathtaking open-chairlift to the top of Monte Solaro for 360-degree views.

Ischia & Procida: If you want to skip the high prices and intense crowds of Capri, visit Ischia for its thermal hot spring spas and beaches, or Procida for a tiny, brightly painted island that still feels like a traditional fishing village.

🏰 The Grand Scale

The Royal Palace of Caserta: Often called Italy’s answer to Versailles, the Reggia di Caserta is a mind-bogglingly massive 18th-century palace. The interior features 1,200 rooms, but the true jaw-dropper is the endless English gardens and cascading fountains stretching for kilometers.

Grok · grok-3

Campania is one of Italy’s most rewarding regions — dramatic landscapes, ancient ruins, world-class food, and some of the most beautiful coastlines in Europe. Here’s a practical guide to the highlights and what to do, depending on how long you have and what you’re into.

Must-do classics

  • Naples – The chaotic, energetic capital. Eat pizza (the real Neapolitan kind), walk the historic centre (Spaccanapoli), visit the National Archaeological Museum (one of the best in the world for Roman artefacts from Pompeii/Herculaneum), and take the funicular up to Vomero for views.
  • Pompeii & Herculaneum – The two famous Roman towns buried by Vesuvius in 79 AD. Pompeii is larger and more famous; Herculaneum is smaller, better preserved, and often less crowded. Do both if you can. Combine with a visit to Mount Vesuvius itself (easy bus + short hike to the crater).
  • Amalfi Coast – The iconic stretch of coast with Positano, Amalfi, and Ravello. Best experienced by boat, bus (the famous SITA buses), or a mix of both. Positano is the postcard town; Ravello has the best views and quieter gardens (Villa Rufolo, Villa Cimbrone).
  • Capri – Day trip from Naples or Sorrento. Famous for the Blue Grotto, the Piazzetta, and the walk up to Monte Solaro. Can feel touristy in high season, but the cliffs and sea are spectacular.
  • Sorrento – Great base for the Amalfi Coast and Capri. Lively but more relaxed than Positano. Excellent lemon products (limoncello) and views across the bay to Vesuvius.

Beyond the obvious

  • Paestum – Greek temples (some of the best-preserved in the world) south of Salerno. Much quieter than Pompeii and beautiful in the late afternoon light. Pair with mozzarella di bufala from nearby farms.
  • Cilento National Park – Wilder, less touristy coastline and hills south of the Amalfi Coast. Great for hiking, swimming in quieter coves, and eating simple, excellent seafood.
  • Ischia – Larger and greener than Capri. Famous for thermal spas, gardens (La Mortella is outstanding), and a more local feel. Good for a multi-day stay.
  • Procida – The smallest and most colourful of the islands in the Bay of Naples. Quiet, authentic, great for a relaxed day or overnight.
  • Caserta – The enormous Royal Palace (Reggia di Caserta) – Italy’s answer to Versailles, with huge gardens. Easy day trip from Naples by train.
  • Wine & countryside – The hills around Avellino and Benevento produce excellent wines (Fiano, Greco di Tufo, Taurasi). Some estates offer tastings and stays.

Food you should prioritise

  • Pizza in Naples (especially places that still use wood-fired ovens and San Marzano tomatoes)
  • Mozzarella di bufala (best near Paestum or Caserta)
  • Seafood pasta, especially with clams or mussels
  • Sfogliatella and babà for pastry
  • Limoncello and local wines (Greco di Tufo, Fiano di Avellino, Aglianico)
  • Street food in Naples: fried pizza, cuoppo, etc.

Practical tips

  • Best time: April–June and September–October. July–August is hot, crowded, and more expensive on the coast.
  • Getting around: Trains are excellent for Naples–Pompeii–Sorrento–Salerno. For the Amalfi Coast, buses or boats are better than driving (the road is narrow and stressful). Ferries connect Naples, Sorrento, Capri, Ischia, and Procida.
  • Base options:
    • Naples if you want urban energy and easy day trips
    • Sorrento as a convenient coastal hub
    • Positano or Amalfi if you want to be right on the famous coast (book early)
    • Salerno or the Cilento for a quieter, more local experience

Suggested short itineraries

  • 3–4 days: Naples + Pompeii/Herculaneum + day trip to Capri or the Amalfi Coast
  • 5–7 days: Naples + Pompeii + 2–3 nights on the Amalfi Coast or Capri + Ischia or Paestum
  • 10+ days: Add Cilento, the islands properly, wine country, and some slower time in smaller towns
